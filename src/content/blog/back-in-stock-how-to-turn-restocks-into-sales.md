When a product is out of stock, the sale is not necessarily lost. Many shoppers leave because they can’t buy the product _right now_, not because they have lost interest. A back-in-stock notification gives your store a way to stay connected and bring those shoppers back when the product becomes available again.

The key is to treat a restock as more than an inventory update. **It is a new opportunity to reach customers who have already shown interest and are much closer to buying than a completely new visitor.** In this guide, we’ll explain how back-in-stock notifications work, why they matter, and how Shopify stores can use them to turn restocks into more sales.

* * *

## What are back-in-stock notifications?

A back-in-stock notification is a message sent to a customer when a product they wanted becomes available again. Usually, the process starts on an out-of-stock product page. Instead of simply showing a **Sold out** message, the store gives shoppers a way to request a notification.

For example:

**Product is out of stock → Shopper clicks “Notify me” → Shopper enters their contact information → Product is restocked → Notification is sent automatically**

This creates a direct connection between your inventory and customers who have already shown interest in a specific product. Back-in-stock notifications are especially valuable because the customer has already done some of the work for you. They found the product, considered buying it, and actively asked to be notified when it returned.

* * *

## Why back-in-stock notifications can drive strong results

Most marketing campaigns are designed to **create interest**. Back-in-stock notifications are different. They help you capture interest that already exists.

A shopper who signs up for a restock alert has usually shown a much stronger level of intent than someone receiving a general promotional email. They already know which product they want. This makes restock notifications useful in several ways.

### 1\. Recover potential sales

Without a notification option, an out-of-stock product can become the end of the customer journey. The shopper may leave your store, search for the same product elsewhere, and eventually buy from a competitor.

A simple notification gives them a reason to return instead. When the product becomes available, your store can immediately bring the conversation back to the product they originally wanted.

### 2\. Create a better shopping experience

Customers don’t want to repeatedly visit your website to check whether a product has returned. A notification saves them time and removes that extra effort. Instead of making the customer remember your store, **your store remembers their interest and contacts them at the right moment**.

### 3\. Learn which products customers really want

Every restock request is also useful demand data. If hundreds of customers request a notification for one product while only a few care about another, that tells you something important about future inventory decisions.

You can use restock demand to identify popular products, understand which variants are in demand, and plan future purchasing more confidently.

## How the back-in-stock customer journey works

A good back-in-stock strategy should feel simple from the customer’s perspective. Behind the scenes, however, several steps need to work together.

### Step 1: A product becomes unavailable

The customer visits a product page and finds that the item is sold out. This is the critical moment. If the page only says **Out of stock**, many customers will simply leave. Instead, give them a clear next action.

**“Notify me when it’s back”** is simple, easy to understand, and directly connected to what the customer wants.

* * *

### Step 2: The customer requests a notification

The signup process should be quick. In most cases, asking for an email address is enough. If your store also uses web push notifications, customers may be able to subscribe to browser notifications without needing to provide an email address.

Try to avoid unnecessary fields. The more information customers need to enter, the more likely they are to abandon the notification signup itself. A short and simple process creates less friction.

* * *

### Step 3: Your system detects the restock

Once inventory becomes available again, the notification should be triggered automatically. Speed matters here. Customer intent does not stay the same forever. Someone who was highly interested last week may already be looking at another product today. The sooner you can notify interested shoppers after a genuine restock, the better your chances of bringing them back.

* * *

### Step 4: The customer receives the notification

The message should immediately answer one question:

**“Is the product I wanted available now?”**

Make that information obvious. Include the product name, a recognizable product image, and a clear call-to-action button that takes the customer directly back to the product page. Don’t make them search through your store again.

* * *

## The most important part: Notify customers about the right product

A common mistake with restock notifications is treating all product availability as the same.

Imagine a customer wanted:

**Black sneakers — Size 9**

If only Size 7 becomes available again, that is not necessarily useful to the customer. The same problem applies to colors, styles, bundles, and other product variants. A reliable back-in-stock system should be as specific as possible. Customers should ideally receive a notification when **the exact product or variant they wanted** becomes available.

This accuracy also builds trust. If customers repeatedly receive notifications for products they can’t actually buy, they may stop paying attention to future alerts.

## 7 best practices for turning restocks into sales

### 1\. Make the “Notify me” option easy to find

Your notification option should appear exactly where the customer needs it. A good location is near the **Add to cart** button or directly within the out-of-stock area. Don’t hide the option at the bottom of the page. When customers are disappointed that a product is unavailable, you want to immediately show them the next step they can take.

**Simple example:**

> Sold out  
> 🔔 Notify me when available

The message is clear and requires almost no explanation.

* * *

### 2\. Send the notification quickly

A restock alert is highly time-sensitive. The customer wanted the product when they signed up, so the notification should arrive while that interest is still fresh. Avoid manually collecting requests and sending a campaign days later. Automation is much more effective because the message can be triggered as soon as the product is genuinely available.

**The basic rule is simple:**

> Product becomes available → Notification is triggered → Customer can return and buy

The shorter the gap between those steps, the smoother the experience becomes.

* * *

### 3\. Show the product clearly

Customers may have looked at dozens of products since they last visited your store. Don’t assume they will immediately remember the item. Include a clear product image and product name so they can instantly recognize what the notification is about.

A strong message could be as simple as:

**It’s back!**  
_The product you wanted is available again._

Then add a clear button:

**Shop now**

The goal is to reduce the distance between the notification and the purchase.

* * *

### 4\. Add urgency only when it is real

Some popular products sell out quickly after being restocked. If stock is genuinely limited, it can be useful to tell customers.

Examples include:

-   **Back for a limited time**
-   **Popular item — limited stock available**
-   **Only a few left**

However, urgency should be based on reality. Avoid adding pressure simply for the sake of marketing. If every message says _“Last chance!”_, customers will eventually stop believing it.

**Real urgency is more effective because customers can trust it.**

* * *

### 5\. Use the right channel

Email is a natural channel for back-in-stock notifications because customers can receive product details, images, and direct links in one message. However, email isn’t your only option.

**Web push notifications** can also work well when speed is important. A short notification can appear quickly and take the customer directly back to the product.

For example:

> 🔔 Your favorite is back!  
> The item you were waiting for is available again.

A practical approach is to use different channels based on customer preferences and the urgency of the restock.

* * *

### 6\. Send a smart follow-up

Not every customer will buy immediately after the first notification. They may see the message while busy, open it later, or simply forget again. A follow-up can give them another opportunity to return. However, don’t keep sending reminders forever. A simple sequence might look like this:

**Notification 1:** Product is back in stock.

**Notification 2:** Sent later if the customer hasn’t purchased, reminding them that the product is still available.

The second message should add value rather than simply repeat the first one. For example, you could highlight customer reviews, product benefits, or limited availability.

* * *

### 7\. Keep the experience mobile-friendly

Many customers will see your notification on a mobile device. Your message should be easy to understand within a few seconds.

Use:

-   A clear product image
-   Short text
-   A visible call-to-action
-   A button that is easy to tap

Don’t make customers zoom, scroll through a long message, or search for the product link.

**The easier it is to return to the product, the easier it is to complete the purchase.**

* * *

## Email vs web push: Which channel should you use?

Both channels can work for back-in-stock notifications, but they serve slightly different purposes.

|  | **Email** | **Web push** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Best for** | Product details and richer content | Fast reminders |
| **Message length** | Can include more information | Short and direct |
| **Product images** | Works very well | Usually limited |
| **Speed** | Fast | Very immediate |
| **Best use case** | Explaining and selling | Bringing customers back quickly |

For many Shopify stores, the best strategy is not choosing one channel over the other. Instead, use each channel for what it does best. A web push notification can quickly tell customers that the product is available again. An email can provide more product information, social proof, or a follow-up if they don’t return immediately.

This multi-channel approach can help you stay relevant without sending the same message repeatedly through one channel.

## A simple back-in-stock notification sequence

You don’t need a complicated automation to get started. Here is a practical example.

### Notification 1: Immediately after the restock

**Subject:** It’s back in stock!

**Message:**  
Good news — the product you were waiting for is available again.

**CTA:** **Shop now**

The first message should focus on one thing: letting the customer know the product is available.

* * *

### Notification 2: A follow-up for non-buyers

If the customer hasn’t purchased after a reasonable amount of time, send a second reminder.

**Subject:** Still thinking about it?

**Message:**  
The product you wanted is still available. Take another look before it sells out again. You can also add customer reviews, product benefits, or real stock information if relevant.

* * *

### Optional notification 3: Final reminder

Only use a third message when there is a genuine reason. For example, if the product is selling quickly or a limited restock is almost gone, a final reminder can make sense. The goal is not to pressure every customer. The goal is to give genuinely interested shoppers another opportunity to buy.

## What should a good back-in-stock notification include?

Keep the message focused. A strong notification usually includes:

**1\. A clear headline**  
Make it obvious that the product is available again.

**2\. The product name**  
Remind the customer exactly what they wanted.

**3\. A product image**  
Help them recognize the item immediately.

**4\. A direct call-to-action**  
Use a simple button such as **Shop now** or **View product**.

**5\. Real urgency, when relevant**  
Mention limited availability only when it is true.

The notification doesn’t need to be complicated. In fact, shorter messages often work better because the customer already understands the reason for receiving it.

## Common mistakes to avoid

### Sending alerts too late

A notification loses value when it arrives long after the product is available. Customers may have already found another option. Whenever possible, connect your notifications directly to current inventory data.

* * *

### Not checking product variants

Don’t notify a customer that a product is back if the size, color, or version they wanted is still unavailable. Variant-level accuracy is especially important for products with many options.

* * *

### Making customers search for the product again

Every notification should make it easy to return to the product page. A customer should not need to open your homepage and search through your catalog. Use a direct product link.

* * *

### Sending too many reminders

One notification can be helpful. Three or four repeated reminders may become annoying. Create a simple sequence and stop the automation once the customer purchases.

* * *

### Treating restock alerts as only a marketing tool

Back-in-stock notifications can also provide useful business insights. Look at which products receive the most requests. Compare interest across variants. Pay attention to how quickly products sell after customers are notified. This information can help you understand demand, not just send more messages.

* * *

## How to measure whether your back-in-stock strategy is working

Don’t only measure opens or clicks. Those metrics are useful, but the main goal is to understand whether notifications are bringing customers back and generating sales.

Track metrics such as:

### **Notification signup rate**

How many visitors request a notification when a product is unavailable?

### **Open or delivery rate**

Are customers actually receiving and seeing your messages?

### **Click-through rate**

How many customers return to the product page?

### **Conversion rate**

How many notified customers eventually make a purchase?

### **Revenue from restock notifications**

How much revenue can be connected to your back-in-stock automation?

### **Demand by product**

Which products generate the highest number of notification requests? Together, these numbers can help you improve both your marketing and your inventory planning.

The most important thing is to make the process **fast, accurate, and easy**.
