**Black Friday emails are won weeks before the sale, not the week of.** Stores that get the best results from email don’t improvise a few last-minute discounts. They plan ahead.

> **Key dates:** Black Friday is **November 27, 2026** · Cyber Monday is **November 30, 2026**

A strong Black Friday email plan connects **timing, audience, offers, and measurement** into one calendar. This guide gives you an 8-week countdown, a 12-campaign schedule, and everything you need to write and send each email.

If you’re still building your email program, start with our guide on [how to create a Shopify email marketing strategy from scratch](https://uppush.io/how-to-create-a-shopify-email-marketing-strategy-from-scratch/), then come back here.


## 1. Know the key dates

Before you plan anything, lock in the dates that matter.

| Event | Date | Day |
|---|---|---|
| Thanksgiving (US) | November 26 | Thursday |
| **Black Friday** | **November 27** | Friday |
| Small Business Saturday (US) | November 28 | Saturday |
| **Cyber Monday** | **November 30** | Monday |

Not in the US? Online shopping still peaks on Black Friday and Cyber Monday in many markets. Adjust the Thanksgiving references to your own audience.

---

## 2. Make three decisions first

Every campaign in your plan depends on three choices. Make them before you open your email tool.

### Your goal

Pick a number, such as revenue, orders, or new customers.

“More sales” is hard to measure. “200 orders from email” is not.

### Your offer

Choose one clear deal, such as 20% off or a free gift.

Plan a different angle for Cyber Monday so customers have a new reason to return.

### Your calendar

Decide how many campaigns you will send and on which days.

The 12-campaign schedule in section 4 is a good starting point.

## 3. Follow the 8-week countdown

The best Black Friday emails are written weeks before the sale. Here is the full timeline at a glance:

Plan → Protect your inbox placement → Create your template → Write the copy → Build and schedule → Tease → Early access → Launch

> **Reading this late?** If you’re starting after October 5, don’t try to do everything at once. Begin with Week 7 (deliverability), combine Weeks 6 and 5 into one, and aim to have your campaigns scheduled by **November 7**.

### Week 8 (Oct 5–11): Plan

**Goal:** Know what you will send.

- Review last year’s campaigns: best subject lines, best send times, best offers.
- Check that your discount codes and inventory can support the offer.
- Draft your campaign calendar.

### Week 7 (Oct 12–18): Protect your inbox placement

**Goal:** Make sure your campaigns reach the inbox.

- Verify your sending domain and set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.
- Use a custom sender address that customers recognize.
- Remove invalid addresses and long-inactive contacts.

This is the step most stores skip, and it’s the one that can quietly ruin everything else. Read our guide on [Shopify email deliverability](https://uppush.io/shopify-email-deliverability-how-to-keep-your-emails-out-of-spam/) for the full checklist.

### Week 6 (Oct 19–25): Create your template

**Goal:** Build one reusable design.

- Design a template with your logo, a clear headline, one main button, and a visible deadline.
- Keep the layout simple so it loads fast on mobile.
- Prepare a Black Friday page or collection to link to.

### Week 5 (Oct 26–Nov 1): Write the copy

**Goal:** Finish all 12 campaigns in draft.

- Write subject lines and preview text for each campaign.
- Prepare two subject line options for your biggest sends.
- Choose the products and images for each email.

### Week 4 (Nov 2–8): Build and schedule

**Goal:** Have most campaigns ready to go.

- Build the emails from your template.
- Schedule as many as you can by **November 7**.
- Send a test to yourself and check every link on mobile.

### Week 3 (Nov 9–15): Tease

**Goal:** Build anticipation.

- Send your teaser campaign.
- Review the first results: clicks and unsubscribes.
- Fix any problem before the busy days arrive.

### Week 2 (Nov 16–22): Early access

**Goal:** Drive early sales from your best customers.

- Send the early-access campaign.
- Preview your best-selling products.
- Watch stock levels and update emails if items sell out.

### Week 1 (Nov 23–29): Launch

**Goal:** Convert during the peak.

- Send your countdown and launch campaigns.
- Check complaints, unsubscribes, and site speed daily.
- Reply quickly to customer questions.

### Cyber Monday and after (Nov 30–Dec 7)

**Goal:** Finish strong, then thank your customers.

- Launch Cyber Monday with a new angle.
- Send a last-chance campaign with a real deadline.
- Follow up within the week.

## 4. Plan your 12 email campaigns

Twelve campaigns may sound like a lot. But they’re spread across three weeks, and each one has a clear job.

| # | Date | Send to | Purpose | Subject line idea |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nov 10 | All engaged subscribers | Teaser | “Black Friday is coming: here’s what to expect” |
| 2 | Nov 16 | Best customers | Early access opens | “Your early access starts now” |
| 3 | Nov 20 | Early-access list, repeat buyers | Best-seller preview | “Sneak peek: the products going fast” |
| 4 | Nov 23 | All engaged subscribers | Four-day countdown | “4 days to go: set your reminder” |
| 5 | Nov 26 | All engaged subscribers | Starts at midnight | “Starts tonight at midnight” |
| 6 | Nov 27, morning | All engaged subscribers | Launch | “It’s live: Black Friday starts now” |
| 7 | Nov 27, evening | Clicked, did not buy | Gentle reminder | “Still thinking it over? Your picks are waiting” |
| 8 | Nov 28 | All engaged subscribers | Social proof | “Customer favorites, still on sale” |
| 9 | Nov 30, morning | All engaged subscribers | Cyber Monday launch | “Cyber Monday: new deals just dropped” |
| 10 | Nov 30, evening | Non-buyers | Last chance | “Last chance: ends tonight at midnight” |
| 11 | Dec 4 | Buyers | Thank-you and next steps | “Thank you. Here’s what happens with your order” |
| 12 | Dec 7 | Non-buyers | Soft follow-up | “We saved something for you” |

### Each campaign needs three things

- **One goal:** a click, a purchase, or a reminder.
- **One offer:** don’t mix several deals in one email.
- **One main button:** make the next step obvious.

Adjust the days to your time zone and your offer.

## 5. Write each Black Friday email

A good campaign is easy to scan in a few seconds. Use this simple structure:

Subject line → Preview text → Headline → Offer → Products → Button → Deadline → Footer

### Subject line

Keep it short, mention the benefit or the deadline, and avoid all caps.

**A:** Your early access starts now

**B:** Shop Black Friday deals before everyone else

### Preview text

Add what the subject line doesn’t say, such as the offer or the end time.

### Hero section

Show the offer in one line with one strong image.

### Main button

Use a specific action like “Shop the sale”. Repeat it once lower down.

### Featured products

Show three to six products with prices.

### Deadline

State the real end date and time.

### Footer

Include support details, shipping notes, and an unsubscribe link.

Escalate the urgency as the sale goes on: **teaser → live → last chance.**

And never promise a deal you can’t deliver.

## 6. Choose the right send time and frequency

Black Friday inboxes are crowded. Timing and restraint help you stand out.

- **Send launch emails in the morning.** Many shoppers check their phones early.
- **Email engaged readers first.** People who recently opened or clicked can receive more emails.
- **Send fewer emails to cold contacts.** Too many emails to inactive addresses raise complaint rates.
- **Use peak days wisely.** Many stores send two emails each on Black Friday and Cyber Monday.
- **Follow up selectively.** Send reminders only to people who clicked and didn’t buy.

Test what works for your audience, then repeat it next year.

---

## 7. Protect your deliverability

Growing your sends is useful only if your emails reach the inbox. A sudden jump in volume can hurt your sender reputation if you haven’t prepared.

### Follow these basic practices:

- Send only to people who opted in.
- Use your own verified domain.
- Remove or suppress contacts who have been inactive for a long time.
- Make unsubscribing easy.
- Avoid misleading subject lines.
- Monitor bounce, unsubscribe, and complaint rates.

Your goal should be **a smaller engaged audience rather than a large inactive list.**

## 9. Track the metrics that matter

You don’t need to track everything. Start with a short list.

### Click-through rate

Are people engaging with your offers and visiting your store?

### Conversion rate

Are email recipients actually buying?

### Revenue per recipient

How much revenue does each email generate?

### Unsubscribe rate

Are people receiving too many emails?

### Complaint rate

Are you protecting your sender reputation?

Open rates can be less reliable than clicks, so treat them as a rough signal only.

**Compare every campaign with the goal you set in section 2, then improve the next one.**

## 10. Run a final launch check

Even a great plan can fail because of small mistakes. Before the first send, check:

### Content

- Spelling
- Product names
- Prices and discounts
- Dates and deadlines
- Links and discount codes

### Design

- Desktop layout
- Mobile layout
- Images
- Buttons

### Sending

- Sender name and address
- Verified domain
- Audience
- Schedule and time zone

Send yourself a test email and open it on your phone.

## Common Black Friday email mistakes

### 1. Starting too late

Emails written in the last week are rushed and untested.

**Better approach:** Start your countdown at least six to eight weeks before the sale.

### 2. Sending everyone to the homepage

Shoppers should never have to search for the deal.

**Better approach:** Link to the exact product, collection, or sale page.

### 3. Repeating the same offer on Cyber Monday

Customers who ignored the first offer will probably ignore the second.

**Better approach:** Give them a new reason to come back.

### 4. Emailing cold contacts heavily

Too many emails to inactive addresses can damage your sender reputation.

**Better approach:** Send more to engaged subscribers and fewer to inactive ones.

### 5. Skipping deliverability checks

If your emails land in spam, nothing else matters.

**Better approach:** Verify your domain and clean your list before the rush.

### 6. Having no plan after the sale

Many stores stop emailing right after Cyber Monday.

**Better approach:** Thank buyers and keep in touch.

## Final thoughts

A Black Friday email plan doesn’t need to be complicated. The strongest approach is a simple calendar that connects every stage of the sale:

**Plan → Prepare → Tease → Reward early buyers → Launch → Follow up → Thank your customers**

Start early. Fix your deliverability. Send one clear message at a time. Then use your results to improve every campaign that follows.

The principle is the same every season:

> **Send the right message, at the right time, with a clear reason to act.**

That’s what turns Black Friday from a one-day scramble into a repeatable email plan for your Shopify store.
