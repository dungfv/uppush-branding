# Black Friday is here, but your biggest email opportunity may still be ahead of you. Cyber Monday is on Monday, November 30, and most of the work happens this weekend.

Adobe forecasts roughly **$15 billion** in US online spending on Cyber Monday 2026, and Bain expects it to rank among the top five sales days of the year. Those are forecasts, but the signal is clear: shoppers are still buying.

> **Key date:** Cyber Monday is **Monday, November 30, 2026**

The good news is that you don’t need a complicated plan. This guide gives you **three email campaigns** to prepare and send between now and Monday night, with timing, subject lines, and a checklist.

If you haven’t planned your full season yet, see our [Black Friday email marketing plan for Shopify](https://uppush.io/black-friday-email-marketing-plan-for-shopify-2026/).



## 1. Treat Cyber Monday as its own sale

Many stores send the same message on Cyber Monday that they sent on Black Friday. Customers who ignored it once will probably ignore it again.

Cyber Monday works best when it has **its own reason to buy.**

### Give shoppers something new

- An online-only offer
- A new product or bundle
- A free-shipping or free-gift upgrade
- A deeper discount on a smaller selection

You don’t need a bigger discount. You need a **different angle.**



## 2. Prepare this weekend in four steps

Use the next three days to get everything ready.

### Friday (today): Review

- Check which Black Friday emails got clicks.
- Confirm your discount codes and inventory still work.
- Pause any automation that contradicts your sale.

### Saturday: Build

- Write the three campaigns below.
- Prepare your audience lists.
- Choose the products and images.

### Sunday: Test and schedule

- Send a test to yourself and open it on your phone.
- Check every link.
- Schedule the Monday campaigns.

### Monday: Monitor

- Watch clicks, unsubscribes, and complaints.
- Check that your site loads fast.
- Be ready to adjust the last-chance email.



## 3. Campaign 1: The weekend second-chance email

**Goal:** Bring back shoppers who showed interest on Black Friday but didn’t buy.

**Send when:** Saturday, November 28, or Sunday, November 29

**Send to:** Subscribers who clicked but didn’t purchase. Exclude customers who already bought.

### What to include

- A short headline that acknowledges they were interested
- The products they viewed, or your best sellers
- The offer and the end date
- One clear button

### Subject line options

**A:** Still thinking it over? Your picks are waiting 🛍️

**B:** Cyber Monday starts in 24 hours ⏰

### Why it works

These shoppers already know your brand. A gentle reminder costs you almost nothing and often converts better than a broad email to everyone.



## 4. Campaign 2: The Cyber Monday launch

**Goal:** Announce the sale and drive the first wave of orders.

**Send when:** Monday, November 30, in the morning

**Send to:** All engaged subscribers

### What to include

- A clear headline: what’s new and what’s on offer
- A single main offer
- 3 to 6 featured products with prices
- A visible end time
- A main button repeated once lower in the email

### Subject line options

**A:** Cyber Monday is live: new deals inside 🛒

**B:** Online only: today’s offer ends at midnight 🕛

### Make the angle obvious

Lead with what’s different from Black Friday. If the offer is the same, highlight a new collection, a bundle, or free shipping.



## 5. Campaign 3: The last-chance email

**Goal:** Capture late shoppers before the sale ends.

**Send when:** Monday evening, a few hours before the deadline

**Send to:** Subscribers who haven’t purchased. Exclude buyers.

### What to include

- A visible countdown or end time
- A short reminder of the offer
- Best-selling or low-stock products
- One main button

### Subject line options

**A:** Last chance: Cyber Monday ends tonight ⏳

**B:** A few hours left to save 🔔

### Keep the deadline real

If the sale ends at midnight, say so. If you extend it, tell customers clearly and don’t pretend otherwise.

Evening hours are often a busy shopping window, but your own data matters more. Test the send time that works for your audience.


## 6. Your weekend send schedule

| When | Campaign | Send to |
|---|---|---|
| Fri, Nov 27 | Review and prepare | — |
| Sat, Nov 28 or Sun, Nov 29 | Campaign 1: second-chance reminder | Clicked, did not buy |
| Mon, Nov 30, morning | Campaign 2: Cyber Monday launch | All engaged subscribers |
| Mon, Nov 30, evening | Campaign 3: last chance | Non-buyers |
| Tue, Dec 1 | Optional: extended offer or thank-you | Depends on your plan |

Use your customers’ time zone when you schedule.



## 7. Write subject lines that cut through the noise

Cyber Monday inboxes are crowded. Generic lines like “Hurry! Last chance for deals” blend in.

### Be specific

Mention the offer, the deadline, or what’s new.

### Keep it short

Aim for about 50 characters or fewer so it doesn’t get cut off on mobile.

### Support it with preview text

Use the preview to add what the subject line doesn’t say.

**Subject:** Cyber Monday is live: new deals inside 🛒

**Preview:** Online-only offers end at midnight.

### Test on bigger sends

Try two versions of the subject line and keep the winner for next time.



## 8. Choose the right audience

You don’t need complicated targeting. Follow three simple rules:

- **Exclude people who already bought** from your reminder and last-chance emails.
- **Send extra emails to engaged subscribers only.** Too many sends to inactive contacts can hurt your sender reputation.
- **Match the message to the audience.** Shoppers who clicked need a nudge. Everyone else needs a clear reason to visit.

## 10. Track the metrics that matter

You don’t need to track everything. Focus on a short list.

### Click-through rate

Are people interested in your offer?

### Conversion rate

Are recipients actually buying?

### Revenue per recipient

How much revenue does each email generate?

### Unsubscribe rate

Are you sending too often?

### Complaint rate

Are you protecting your sender reputation?

Open rates can be less reliable than clicks, so treat them as a rough signal only.



## Common Cyber Monday email mistakes

### 1. Repeating Black Friday word for word

Customers who ignored the first offer will probably ignore the second.

**Better approach:** Give them a new reason to buy.

### 2. Sending only one email

One morning email is easy to miss in a crowded inbox.

**Better approach:** Send a launch email and a last-chance reminder.

### 3. Emailing people who already bought

It’s annoying, and it can lead to unsubscribes.

**Better approach:** Exclude recent purchasers from reminders.

### 4. Using fake urgency

A deadline that keeps moving damages trust.

**Better approach:** Use a real end time, and be honest if you extend it.

### 5. Forgetting to test

A broken link or expired code can waste your biggest day.

**Better approach:** Test every email on your phone before you schedule it.



## After Cyber Monday

Don’t stop on Tuesday. Thank your buyers and give them a reason to come back.

Thank buyers → Share order information → Ask for a review → Recommend a related product

You don’t need a long campaign to win Cyber Monday. Three focused emails can do the job:

**Second-chance reminder → Cyber Monday launch → Last chance**

Prepare this weekend, use a real deadline, and give shoppers a clear reason to act.

> **Send the right message, at the right time, with a clear reason to act.**

That’s how you turn the busiest online shopping weekend of the year into real results for your Shopify store.
