Email marketing can become one of the most useful channels for a Shopify store, but simply sending occasional promotional emails isn’t a strategy. A good email strategy connects **list building, automation, segmentation, campaigns, and measurement** into one system.

The goal is simple: **turn visitors into subscribers, subscribers into customers, and customers into repeat buyers.** This guide shows you how to build that system from scratch.

* * *

## 1\. Start with a clear email marketing goal

Before choosing an email tool or designing your first campaign, decide what you want email marketing to accomplish.

Different stores may have different priorities.

### Your main goals could include:

-   Generate first purchases from new subscribers
-   Recover abandoned carts and checkouts
-   Increase repeat purchases
-   Promote new products
-   Re-engage inactive customers
-   Build long-term customer relationships

Don’t try to optimize everything at once.

For a new Shopify store, a practical starting point is:

Build your list → Convert subscribers → Recover lost sales → Increase repeat purchases

Once these foundations are working, you can expand into more advanced campaigns.

* * *

## 2\. Choose an email marketing platform

Shopify gives merchants several ways to send emails, from its built-in email tools to dedicated ecommerce email platforms. The right choice depends on your **store size, budget, automation needs, and level of personalization**.

### Look for these core capabilities:

**Shopify integration**  
Your platform should be able to work with customer, order, and product data.

**Automation**  
You should be able to trigger emails based on customer actions.

**Segmentation**  
You need a way to create targeted audiences.

**Analytics**  
The platform should show how campaigns and automations perform.

**Email templates**  
Creating professional emails shouldn’t require advanced design skills.

**Mobile-responsive emails**  
Your emails should remain easy to read and interact with on smaller screens.

Shopify Email can be a straightforward starting point for merchants who need basic campaigns, while platforms such as Klaviyo, Omnisend, Mailchimp, and others offer different levels of automation and segmentation.

### Before choosing a platform, ask:

1.  Does it integrate with Shopify?
2.  Can it track customer behavior?
3.  Can it create automated flows?
4.  Can it segment customers?
5.  Can I measure revenue and conversions?
6.  Will the pricing still work as my list grows?

Choose based on what your store actually needs rather than simply choosing the platform with the longest feature list.

* * *

## 3\. Build your email list

You can’t run an effective email strategy without subscribers. But adding a basic signup form to your footer isn’t enough. Your store should give visitors **clear opportunities to subscribe** throughout the customer journey.

### Common email capture methods

**Popup**

Offer visitors a reason to join your list, such as a discount, free shipping, or useful content.

**Embedded form**

Place signup forms in areas such as your homepage, blog, or other high-traffic pages.

**Checkout opt-in**

Give customers the option to subscribe to marketing communications during checkout where appropriate.

**Landing page**

Create a dedicated page for a specific offer, campaign, or lead magnet.

**Content**

Use guides, product education, quizzes, or other useful resources to give visitors a reason to subscribe.

Top Growth Marketing also recommends combining multiple lead-capture methods rather than relying on one form.

* * *

## 4\. Give people a reason to subscribe

People don’t usually give away their email address without a reason.

Your signup offer should answer one simple question:

> **“Why should I give you my email?”**

Possible incentives include:

-   First-order discount
-   Free shipping
-   Exclusive product access
-   Useful guide or checklist
-   Product recommendations
-   Special promotions
-   Loyalty benefits
-   Early access to launches

The incentive doesn’t always have to be a discount. For some brands, **useful content or exclusive access** can attract better subscribers than a simple coupon. The important thing is to make the benefit clear.

* * *

## 5\. Create a welcome email series

A new subscriber is highly engaged with your brand, so don’t wait until your next promotion to contact them. Create a **welcome automation** that starts after someone subscribes.

A simple series could look like this:

### Email 1 — Welcome

Thank them for subscribing and deliver the promised incentive.

**Goal:** Set expectations and make the first interaction useful.

### Email 2 — Introduce the brand

Explain what your store sells and what makes your products different.

**Goal:** Build familiarity and trust.

### Email 3 — Best sellers

Show products that are popular or relevant to the subscriber.

**Goal:** Help them decide what to buy.

### Email 4 — Social proof

Include reviews, customer stories, ratings, or other trust signals.

**Goal:** Reduce purchase hesitation.

### Email 5 — Final reminder

If you offered a limited-time welcome incentive, remind subscribers before it expires.

**Goal:** Encourage action without repeatedly sending discounts.

A welcome series doesn’t need to be complicated.

The important part is that **new subscribers receive relevant communication immediately instead of entering a silent period**. Both sources emphasize welcome flows as one of the foundational parts of a Shopify email strategy.

* * *

## 6\. Set up your essential email automations

Campaigns are important, but automation is what makes your email strategy work continuously. An automation is triggered by a customer action rather than manually scheduled every time.

For example:

Customer subscribes → Welcome email

Customer abandons cart → Recovery email

Customer purchases → Post-purchase email

Customer becomes inactive → Win-back email

Start with a few high-value automations instead of building dozens of complicated workflows.

## The five core automations

### 1\. Welcome flow

**Trigger:** New subscriber

**Goal:** Convert subscribers into first-time customers.

### 2\. Abandoned cart flow

**Trigger:** Product added to cart but purchase isn’t completed

**Goal:** Recover potential sales.

### 3\. Browse abandonment flow

**Trigger:** Customer views products without purchasing

**Goal:** Bring interested shoppers back.

### 4\. Post-purchase flow

**Trigger:** Customer completes an order

**Goal:** Improve the customer experience and encourage another purchase.

### 5\. Win-back flow

**Trigger:** Customer becomes inactive

**Goal:** Re-engage customers before they are completely lost.

These five flows are repeatedly highlighted in the sources as core ecommerce automations.

* * *

## 7\. Build your abandoned cart flow

Cart recovery deserves special attention because the customer has already shown purchase intent. A simple flow could contain three messages.

### Email 1 — Reminder

Send a simple reminder about the products left in the cart.

**Focus:** Convenience, not pressure.

### Email 2 — Build confidence

Add useful information such as:

-   Customer reviews
-   Product benefits
-   Shipping information
-   Return policy
-   Frequently asked questions

**Focus:** Remove purchase hesitation.

### Email 3 — Final opportunity

If appropriate, provide an incentive or create a clear reason to complete the purchase.

**Focus:** Give the customer one final reason to return.

Most importantly, **stop the automation when the customer completes the purchase**.

There’s no reason to keep sending abandoned-cart emails after someone has already checked out.

* * *

## 8\. Create a post-purchase journey

Your email strategy shouldn’t stop when the order is completed. The first purchase creates an opportunity to build a longer relationship.

A basic post-purchase sequence can include:

**Order confirmation**

→ Confirm the purchase and provide important information.

**Shipping update**

→ Keep the customer informed.

**Product education**

→ Explain how to use, care for, or get more value from the product.

**Review request**

→ Ask for feedback after the customer has had enough time with the product.

**Product recommendation**

→ Recommend something related when the timing makes sense.

**Loyalty or referral**

→ Introduce rewards or referral opportunities when appropriate.

This helps move the customer from:

**First purchase → Good experience → Second purchase → Repeat customer**

Top Growth Marketing also recommends post-purchase communication as one of the essential Shopify email workflows.

* * *

## 9\. Segment your customers

Sending the same email to your entire database is rarely the best approach. Your subscribers are at different stages and have different interests. Start with a few practical segments.

### New subscribers

**No purchase yet**

Send welcome content, best sellers, reviews, and first-purchase incentives.

### First-time buyers

**One purchase**

Send product education, complementary products, and second-purchase campaigns.

### Repeat customers

**Multiple purchases**

Send new products, loyalty benefits, and personalized recommendations.

### VIP customers

**High spend or high purchase frequency**

Give them early access, exclusive benefits, or special experiences.

### Inactive customers

**No purchase or engagement for a defined period**

Use win-back campaigns.

### Product or category interests

**Customers interested in a specific product category**

Send campaigns focused on products they are more likely to care about. Segmentation based on purchase history, browsing behavior, and customer characteristics is a core part of long-term Shopify email marketing.

* * *

## 10\. Create regular email campaigns

Automations handle individual customer journeys.

**Campaigns handle your broader marketing calendar.**

These are emails you intentionally send to a selected audience at a specific time.

### Useful campaign types include:

**Product launches**  
Introduce new products or collections.

**Promotions**  
Share sales, seasonal offers, or special events.

**Educational content**  
Teach customers something useful related to your products.

**Customer stories**  
Show reviews, UGC, or real customer experiences.

**Product recommendations**  
Highlight products based on customer interests.

**Seasonal campaigns**  
Connect your products with events, holidays, or shopping seasons.

**Behind-the-scenes content**  
Show how products are made or introduce the people behind your brand.

The goal isn’t to email customers every time you have something to sell.

A stronger strategy mixes **promotional and useful content** so subscribers have a reason to continue opening your emails.

* * *

## 11\. Create an email marketing calendar

Without a calendar, email marketing can become inconsistent. You might send three emails in one week and then disappear for a month. A simple calendar helps balance automated flows with regular campaigns.

### Example monthly structure

**Week 1**

Educational email + product recommendation

**Week 2**

Product-focused campaign

**Week 3**

Customer story or useful content

**Week 4**

Promotion or seasonal campaign Your automations continue running in the background throughout the month.

This creates two layers:

**Automations = behavior-based communication**

**Campaigns = planned communication**

Keeping these two systems separate makes your email strategy easier to manage.

## 12\. Create a consistent email design

Your email should feel like it belongs to the same brand as your Shopify store.

Use consistent:

-   Logo
-   Colors
-   Typography
-   Product imagery
-   Button styles
-   Tone of voice

But don’t make the design unnecessarily complicated.

A good ecommerce email should make it easy to understand:

What is this about?

Why does it matter to me?

What should I do next?

### A simple structure

Headline

↓

Short explanation

↓

Product or visual

↓

Key benefit

↓

CTA

↓

Supporting information

Top Growth Marketing recommends keeping email layouts clean, mobile-responsive, and aligned with the Shopify store’s visual identity.

* * *

## 13\. Make every email mobile-friendly

A beautiful desktop email can still perform poorly if it is difficult to use on a phone.

Before sending, check:

-   Is the text easy to read?
-   Are buttons large enough to tap?
-   Are images loading correctly?
-   Is the main message visible quickly?
-   Can customers understand the email without excessive scrolling?
-   Does the layout work across different screen sizes?

Keep your writing concise. Instead of putting everything into one long email, use **short sections, clear headings, and one primary CTA**. Mobile optimization should be part of your normal email workflow, not something you check only after a campaign performs badly.

* * *

## 14\. Use personalization carefully

Personalization doesn’t mean putting someone’s first name at the top of every email. Useful personalization is based on **what the customer actually cares about**.

For example:

**Purchase history**

“You bought our cleanser. Here’s a moisturizer that pairs with it.”

**Browsing behavior**

“You were checking out this collection. Here are three popular options.”

**Customer lifecycle**

“You’ve been shopping with us for a year. Here’s early access to our new collection.”

**Location**

“Your local holiday sale starts this weekend.”

The principle is simple:

> **Personalize the message when you have useful information to personalize it with.**

## 15\. Test your emails before sending

Even a good strategy can fail because of small mistakes.

Before launching a campaign or automation, test:

### Content

-   Spelling
-   Product names
-   Prices
-   Discounts
-   Dates
-   Links

### Design

-   Desktop layout
-   Mobile layout
-   Images
-   Buttons
-   Fonts

### Automation

-   Trigger
-   Timing
-   Audience
-   Exit conditions
-   Purchase suppression

### Personalization

-   Customer name
-   Product information
-   Dynamic content
-   Recommendation blocks

Send yourself a test email before activating the workflow.

This is especially important for automated emails because a mistake can continue repeating until someone notices it.

## 16\. Use A/B testing to improve performance

You don’t need to test everything at once. Start with the elements that can have the biggest effect.

### Test subject lines

**A:** Your cart is waiting

**B:** Still thinking about these?

### Test CTAs

**A:** Shop now

**B:** Complete your order

### Test content

Compare:

-   Product-focused emails
-   Educational emails
-   Customer stories
-   Promotional emails

### Test offers

Compare different incentives when they make business sense. The important part is to **change one meaningful variable at a time** so you can understand what caused the difference. Top Growth Marketing recommends testing subject lines, content formats, CTAs, and product recommendations as part of ongoing optimization.

* * *

## 17\. Track the metrics that matter

Email marketing generates a lot of data. You don’t need to track everything. Start with a small set of metrics.

### List growth rate

Are you gaining subscribers faster than you’re losing them?

### Click-through rate

Are people engaging with the content and visiting your store?

### Conversion rate

Are email recipients actually purchasing or completing the desired action?

### Revenue per recipient

How much revenue does each recipient generate?

### Unsubscribe rate

Are people losing interest or receiving too many irrelevant emails?

### Flow revenue

Which automations are actually contributing to sales?

The goal isn’t to maximize one metric.

**Use multiple metrics together to understand whether your emails are attracting, engaging, and converting the right customers.**

* * *

## 18\. Protect email deliverability

Growing your list is useful only if your emails reach the inbox. Poor list quality, excessive sending, and irrelevant messages can hurt engagement and deliverability.

### Follow these basic practices:

-   Send to people who have opted in
-   Keep your signup process clear
-   Remove or suppress consistently inactive contacts when appropriate
-   Make unsubscribing easy
-   Avoid misleading subject lines
-   Monitor bounce and unsubscribe rates
-   Don’t send every campaign to your entire database

Your goal should be **a smaller engaged audience rather than a large inactive list**. Deliverability should be considered from the beginning of your email strategy, not after emails start landing in spam.

## Common Shopify email marketing mistakes

### 1\. Sending only promotional emails

If every email says “20% off,” customers have little reason to stay engaged.

**Better approach:** Mix promotions with education, product discovery, customer stories, and useful content.

### 2\. Building a large list without a strategy

More subscribers don’t automatically mean more revenue.

**Better approach:** Focus on collecting relevant, opted-in subscribers and keeping them engaged.

### 3\. Starting with complicated automation

You don’t need 20 flows on day one.

**Better approach:** Start with welcome, abandoned cart, and post-purchase flows.

### 4\. Sending the same email to everyone

Customers have different interests and buying histories.

**Better approach:** Use segmentation.

### 5\. Ignoring mobile

Many customers read emails on their phones.

**Better approach:** Keep layouts simple, text concise, and buttons easy to tap.

### 6\. Never testing

A campaign can look good but still perform poorly.

**Better approach:** Test subject lines, content, CTAs, and offers over time.

### 7\. Measuring only opens

An email can have a high open rate and generate very little business value.

**Better approach:** Look at clicks, conversions, revenue, and customer behavior.

## Final thoughts

A Shopify email marketing strategy doesn’t need to be complicated. The strongest foundation is a simple system that connects every stage of the customer journey:

**Capture subscribers → Welcome them → Convert them → Recover lost purchases → Follow up after purchase → Bring inactive customers back → Optimize continuously**

Start with the basics. Build your list. Set up your core automations. Segment your customers. Send useful campaigns. Then use your data to improve what you already have. As your store grows, your email strategy can become more sophisticated.

But the foundation remains the same:

> **Send the right message, to the right customer, at the right time.**

That is what turns email from an occasional promotional channel into a repeatable marketing system for your Shopify store.
