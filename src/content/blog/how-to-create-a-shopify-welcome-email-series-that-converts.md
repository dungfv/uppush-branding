A new subscriber has just given you something valuable: **permission to contact them**. What happens next can determine whether they become a customer or simply disappear from your list.

A welcome email series gives you a chance to introduce your brand, deliver the value you promised, answer early questions, and guide subscribers toward their first purchase. Instead of sending one generic “Welcome” email, a short sequence can build the relationship step by step.

The key is not to send more emails just for the sake of sending more. **Each email should have a clear purpose and move the customer one step closer to buying.** This guide explains how to build a Shopify welcome email series, what to include in each email, when to send it, and how to improve its performance over time.

* * *

## Why a welcome email series matters

The welcome email is often the **first marketing message** a new subscriber receives from your store. It sets expectations for what your future emails will look like and gives customers their first real impression of your brand. Shopify also notes that welcome emails tend to receive stronger engagement than regular marketing emails.

But the value of a welcome series goes beyond the first email. A new subscriber may be interested in your products but still have questions about quality, pricing, shipping, returns, or your brand. A sequence gives you enough space to address those questions **without trying to fit everything into one message**.

A simple way to think about it is:

> **Email 1 gets attention → Email 2 builds interest → Email 3 removes doubts → Email 4 builds trust → Email 5 creates a final reason to act.**

The exact number of emails can vary by store, but this progression gives each message a specific job.

* * *

## Before you build the series: start with the signup

Your welcome series actually begins **before the first email is sent**. When someone subscribes through a popup, signup form, or another offer, the promise you make at that moment should match what they receive afterward.

For example, if your popup says:

> **Get $15 off your first order**

the first email should deliver that $15 offer immediately.

If your popup promises:

> **Get our skincare guide**

the first email should provide the guide rather than immediately pushing products.

This is important because **the signup creates an expectation**. Breaking that expectation can reduce trust before the welcome series has even started.

### Keep your signup offer clear

A strong signup form should make three things easy to understand:

1.  **What the customer gets**
2.  **What they need to do**
3.  **What happens next**

Shopify recommends making the value of the welcome offer clear and setting expectations about future communication.

* * *

## A 5-email Shopify welcome series

A five-email sequence can work well for stores that want to move beyond a simple welcome message. The sequence below follows a logical progression: **deliver the promise → introduce the brand → answer objections → provide proof → create a final reason to buy.**

You don’t have to copy the sequence exactly. The important part is that **each email has a different purpose**.

* * *

### 1\. Email #1: Deliver the promise

**Timing: Immediately after signup**

The first email should arrive as soon as possible after the subscriber signs up. If you promised a discount, guide, free resource, or another benefit, **deliver it immediately**. Shopify specifically recommends automating the welcome email so it is triggered as soon as the related action takes place.

#### What should this email include?

Keep it simple:

-   A friendly welcome
-   The promised offer or resource
-   A short introduction to the brand
-   One clear CTA
-   A link to a relevant product or collection

Don’t try to explain your entire business in the first email. The subscriber has just joined. Your priority is to **keep the promise and give them an easy next step**.

#### Example

**Subject:** Welcome to \[Brand\] — your $15 gift is here

> Thanks for joining us!  
> Here’s your $15 welcome gift to use on your first order.
> 
> Ready to explore? Start with our best sellers.
> 
> **\[Shop best sellers\]**

Notice that there is one obvious action. Shopify recommends using a clear CTA rather than surrounding the reader with multiple competing actions.

> 💡 **Pro tip:** If you offer a discount, consider sending customers to a **curated collection or best-seller page** instead of simply sending them to your homepage. Give them an obvious place to start shopping.

* * *

### 2\. Email #2: Tell your brand story

**Timing: About 24 hours later**

Once you’ve delivered the initial offer, the next email can focus less on selling and more on **why your brand exists**. Customers don’t always buy because a product is cheaper. They also buy because they understand the brand, trust its values, or connect with the problem it is trying to solve.

Shopify’s welcome email examples show several ways brands use their first communications to introduce their story, personality, mission, and product positioning without overwhelming the reader.

#### What should you talk about?

Answer three simple questions:

**Why does your brand exist?**

Explain the problem or opportunity that led you to create the business.

**Who is your product for?**

Help subscribers recognize themselves as the type of customer your products are designed for.

**What makes your product different?**

Focus on one or two meaningful differences instead of listing every feature.

#### Keep the story short

You don’t need to write your entire company history. A short founder message, a few strong product images, or a simple explanation of your mission can be enough to make the brand feel more human.

> 💡 **Pro tip:** Write this email like you’re talking to **one customer**, not presenting your company to an audience. A natural “we” and “you” usually feels more personal than corporate language.

* * *

### 3\. Email #3: Answer the biggest buying question

**Timing: Around 48–72 hours after signup**

If a subscriber has received two emails but still hasn’t purchased, there may be something holding them back. The problem isn’t always lack of interest. They may simply have an unanswered question:

Will it fit?

Is shipping expensive?

What happens if I don’t like it?

Does this product actually work**?**

This email should identify the **biggest objection in your buying journey** and answer it directly.

### Match the content to your product

For fashion:

> **Not sure about the fit?**  
> Show your size guide, real customer measurements, or fit recommendations.

For beauty:

> **Not sure which product to choose?**  
> Explain which product is best for different skin types or needs.

For higher-priced products:

> **Worried about making the wrong choice?**  
> Explain your return policy, warranty, customer support, or product guarantee.

For products with complicated features:

> **Not sure how it works?**  
> Show a simple demonstration or explain the product in everyday language.

This email is less about selling and more about **removing uncertainty**.

> 💡 **Pro tip:** Look at your customer support questions, product reviews, and return reasons. They can tell you exactly which objections your welcome series should address.

* * *

### 4\. Email #4: Build trust with social proof

**Timing: About 24 hours after Email #3**

At this point, the subscriber may understand your brand and product but still need reassurance. This is where **social proof** becomes useful. Instead of telling customers that your product is great, show them that other customers have already bought it and had a positive experience.

#### What can you include?

-   Customer reviews
-   Star ratings
-   User-generated photos
-   Short testimonials
-   Best-selling products
-   Customer numbers
-   Awards or relevant media coverage

The strongest testimonials are specific.

Compare:

> “Love this product!”

with:

> “I bought this for my first hiking trip and it kept my feet comfortable for eight hours.”

The second example gives the reader **a reason to believe the product will work for them**.

Shopify’s examples also show how customer stories, reviews, and brand credibility can be incorporated into welcome emails to strengthen trust.

> 💡 **Pro tip:** Don’t fill the email with twenty reviews. Choose **one or two strong examples** that address the same concern you discussed in Email #3.

* * *

### 5\. Email #5: Create a final reason to act

**Timing: Before the welcome offer expires**

The final email is for subscribers who haven’t purchased yet. If you offered a discount with an actual expiration date, this is a natural place to remind them about it. The important word is **actual**. Don’t say “your offer expires tonight” if you’re going to extend it tomorrow. False urgency can damage trust and teach customers to ignore your deadlines.

#### What should this email include?

Bring the most important information together:

-   The original offer
-   The main product or best sellers
-   One strong customer review
-   Important reassurance such as returns or shipping
-   A clear CTA
-   The real expiration date

The subscriber shouldn’t have to go back through four previous emails to understand what they’re getting.

#### Example

**Subject:** Your $15 welcome gift expires tomorrow

> Your welcome gift is still waiting.
> 
> Save $15 on your first order before the offer expires tomorrow.
> 
> **\[Use my $15 gift\]**

Keep the final email **short and direct**.

> 💡 **Pro tip:** If someone purchases during Email #1 or #2, remove them from the remaining welcome sequence. A customer shouldn’t continue receiving “first purchase” messages after they’ve already bought.

* * *

## How the five emails work together

The strength of a welcome series comes from how the emails **work as a sequence**, not from any single message.

| Email | Main purpose | What the customer should get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1\. The promise** | Deliver value | Offer + clear next step |
| **2\. The story** | Build connection | Why your brand exists |
| **3\. The reason** | Remove objections | Answers to buying concerns |
| **4\. The proof** | Build confidence | Reviews and customer evidence |
| **5\. The last call** | Encourage action | Final reminder or genuine urgency |

The progression is simple:

**Welcome → Understand → Trust → Believe → Buy**

This prevents the sequence from becoming five versions of the same promotional email.

## How long should you wait between emails?

There is no single timing schedule that works for every Shopify store. A customer buying a $20 product may make a decision within hours. Someone considering a $500 product may need several days. Still, a starting structure could look like:

```
Signup
  ↓
Email 1 — Immediately
  ↓
24 hours
  ↓
Email 2 — Brand story
  ↓
48 hours
  ↓
Email 3 — Objection
  ↓
24 hours
  ↓
Email 4 — Social proof
  ↓
24–48 hours
  ↓
Email 5 — Final reminder
```

The second source uses a seven-day, five-email structure with the messages moving from the initial offer to brand story, objection handling, proof, and a final call. Treat this as a **starting framework**, not a fixed rule. Your own data should determine whether subscribers need more or less time.

## Personalize the welcome series based on how people subscribe

Not every subscriber has the same level of intent. Someone who signs up from a product page may already be interested in a specific product. Someone who joins through a general homepage popup may simply be curious about the brand. If possible, use the signup source or behavior to make the welcome series more relevant.

### For example:

**Product-page signup**

> “Interested in our running shoes? Here’s how to choose the right pair.”

**Homepage signup**

> “Welcome to \[Brand\]. Here’s what makes our products different.”

**Guide or content signup**

> “Here’s the guide you requested. You may also find these products useful.”

The second source recommends segmenting welcome flows by where the subscriber came from, particularly when the source indicates different levels of purchase intent. You don’t need dozens of segments. Start with **two or three meaningful groups** and expand only when the data shows that the difference matters.

## What should you write in the subject line?

Your subject line is the first thing the subscriber sees, so it needs to make the purpose of the email clear.

Avoid relying on generic lines such as:

> “Welcome to our newsletter!”

Instead, make the subject relevant to what the customer is receiving.

### Offer-focused

-   Your $15 welcome gift is inside
-   Here’s your 10% off
-   Your welcome offer is ready

### Brand-focused

-   Welcome to \[Brand\]
-   Why we started \[Brand\]
-   Meet the people behind \[Brand\]

### Helpful

-   Not sure which one to choose?
-   Here’s how to find the right fit
-   3 things to know before you buy

### Final reminder

-   Your welcome offer expires tomorrow
-   Last day to use your welcome gift

Shopify recommends testing subject lines and notes that curiosity, urgency, and a sense of belonging are common themes in effective welcome emails.

* * *

## Keep one clear CTA in each email

One of the easiest ways to make a welcome email confusing is to give the customer too many choices.

If Email #3 is about choosing the right product, the CTA could simply be:

**\[Find the right product\]**

If Email #4 is focused on reviews:

**\[See customer reviews\]**

If Email #5 is about the welcome offer:

**\[Use my welcome gift\]**

Shopify recommends giving each welcome email a clear next action and keeping the CTA visually easy to find.

The customer should never have to ask:

> _“What am I supposed to do after reading this?”_

## Common mistakes to avoid

### **1\. Sending only one email**

One email can work for a very simple store, but it leaves little room to introduce your brand, answer questions, and build trust. If your subscribers need more consideration before buying, a short series gives you more opportunities to help them.

### **2\. Making every email a sales pitch**

Five emails that all say **“Buy now!”** will quickly become repetitive. Give each email a different job: **value, story, education, proof, then conversion.**

### **3\. Sending the first email too late**

If you promise an offer immediately but make customers wait hours for it, the experience starts badly. Automate Email #1 so it is triggered immediately after signup.

### **4\. Putting everything into Email #1**

Don’t explain your company history, show twenty products, list every benefit, and include five CTAs in the first message. **Keep the first email focused.** You have several more emails to tell the story.

### **5\. Forgetting mobile users**

Shopify notes that a significant share of emails are opened on mobile devices, making mobile-friendly design important. Test your emails on different screen sizes before launching the series.

### **6\. Not stopping the flow after purchase**

If someone purchases after Email #1, they should not receive Email #2 telling them to make their first purchase. Add a **purchase exit condition** and move buyers into your post-purchase journey instead.

## How to measure your welcome email series

Don’t judge the entire series using one metric. Look at the numbers at different stages of the journey.

### **Open rate**

Shows whether subscribers are opening your emails. A low open rate can point to problems with the subject line, sender name, timing, or deliverability.

### **Click-through rate**

Shows whether the email is encouraging subscribers to take the next step. If people open an email but rarely click, the content or CTA may need improvement.

### **Conversion rate**

This is the most important metric when the goal is first purchases. Track how many subscribers ultimately place an order after entering the welcome flow.

### **Revenue per recipient**

This can help you understand how much revenue the entire flow generates relative to the number of people who receive it.

### **Unsubscribe rate**

A sudden increase can indicate that your emails are too frequent, too promotional, or different from what subscribers expected. Shopify recommends monitoring metrics such as open rate, CTR, conversion rate, unsubscribe rate, A/B testing results, and deliverability when evaluating welcome email performance.

* * *

## Test one thing at a time

Your first version won’t necessarily be your best version. Once the welcome flow has enough data, test individual elements rather than changing everything at once.

You can test:

-   **Subject line**
-   Sender name
-   Discount amount
-   Email timing
-   CTA wording
-   Product selection
-   Brand story angle
-   Customer review placement

For example, test:

> **Your $15 gift is inside**

against:

> **Welcome to \[Brand\] — here’s $15 for you**

If the second version generates more purchases, you have learned something useful about what your audience responds to.

Shopify also recommends A/B testing subject lines, content, and CTAs to identify what performs better.

> **Don’t optimize for opens alone.** A subject line that gets more opens but fewer purchases isn’t necessarily a better subject line.

## Final thoughts

A Shopify welcome email series doesn’t need to be complicated. The most important thing is to stop thinking of it as **five promotional emails** and start thinking of it as one customer journey.

Email 1 delivers the promise.

Email 2 introduces the brand.

Email 3 removes a buying objection.

Email 4 builds trust through proof.

Email 5 gives the customer a final reason to act.

From there, use your data to improve the sequence. Test the timing, subject lines, offers, CTAs, and content that matter most to your customers.

The goal isn’t to send more emails.

> **The goal is to make every email useful enough that the customer wants to receive the next one.**
