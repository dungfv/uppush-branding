Sending the same email to every customer may be simple, but it doesn’t mean the message is relevant to everyone. A new subscriber, a first-time buyer, a loyal customer, and someone who hasn’t purchased in months are at very different stages of the customer journey.

**Shopify email segmentation** helps you group customers based on shared characteristics or behaviors, so you can send each group more relevant messages. Instead of asking, _“What should we send to everyone?”_, segmentation changes the question to:

> **“What does this specific group need right now?”**

* * *

## What is Shopify email segmentation?

Email segmentation is the process of dividing your customer list into smaller groups based on information such as **purchase history, behavior, interests, location, or engagement**.

For example, you could create separate segments for:

-   Customers who have never purchased
-   First-time buyers
-   Repeat customers
-   High-value customers
-   Customers who recently purchased
-   Customers who haven’t purchased recently
-   Shoppers interested in a specific product category
-   Subscribers who rarely engage with your emails

Shopify’s customer segments can use multiple filters, and segments can update automatically as customers meet or stop meeting the criteria. This means segmentation doesn’t have to be a collection of manually maintained lists.

**Customer behavior changes → segment changes → marketing can change with it.**

* * *

## Why email segmentation matters for Shopify stores

The main benefit of segmentation is simple:

**More relevant customers receive more relevant messages.**

A customer who just purchased running shoes probably doesn’t need the same email as someone who has never bought from your store.

Segmentation helps you improve three areas:

### 1\. Relevance

Messages can reflect what customers have actually done.

For example:

Bought skincare → recommend related skincare products

Abandoned checkout → remind them about the products they left behind

Hasn’t purchased in 6 months → send a win-back campaign

### 2\. Customer experience

Customers receive fewer messages that don’t apply to them. Instead of constantly receiving general promotions, they can receive content based on their interests and relationship with your store.

### 3\. Marketing efficiency

A smaller, highly relevant audience can be more useful than sending every campaign to your entire database. Shopify specifically supports using customer segments for targeted email campaigns, including product recommendations, re-engagement, loyalty offers, and location-specific promotions.

* * *

## The 7 most useful Shopify email segments

You don’t need dozens of segments to get started. For most Shopify stores, these **seven groups** cover the most important customer situations.

* * *

### 1\. New subscribers who haven’t purchased

These customers have shown interest in your brand but haven’t placed an order yet. They’re usually at the **top of the customer journey**, so the goal isn’t to immediately push every product you sell.

### Send them:

-   A welcome email
-   Your best-selling products
-   Customer reviews
-   Your brand story
-   Answers to common questions
-   A first-purchase incentive, if appropriate

#### Example

**Segment:** Email subscribers + 0 orders

**Message:**  
“New here? Start with our most-loved products.”

The content should reduce uncertainty and make the first purchase easier. This segment can also feed directly into a **welcome email automation**.

* * *

### 2\. First-time buyers

A customer who has made one purchase is different from someone who has never bought. They already know your brand, so your goal changes from **first conversion** to **second purchase and long-term retention**.

### Send them:

-   Product education
-   Usage tips
-   Related products
-   Review requests
-   Replenishment reminders
-   A second-purchase offer when appropriate

### Example

**Segment:** 1 order + recent purchase

**Message:**  
“Love your new product? Here’s how to get even more from it.”

The first few emails should focus on helping the customer succeed with their purchase. Once the timing is right, introduce products that naturally complement what they bought.

* * *

### 3\. Repeat customers

Repeat customers have already demonstrated that they are willing to buy from you again. That makes them a valuable group to nurture. But don’t assume that every repeat customer wants another discount.

### Try:

-   New product announcements
-   Product recommendations
-   Early access
-   Loyalty rewards
-   Exclusive content
-   Cross-sell campaigns

#### Example

A customer who has purchased from your skincare category three times could receive:

**“New skincare products you might like.”**

Instead of:

**“20% off everything in our store.”**

The first message is based on **known interest**. The second assumes that every product is equally relevant.

* * *

### 4\. High-value customers

Not every customer contributes the same amount of revenue. A customer who has placed 8 orders and spent $1,500 deserves a different communication strategy from someone who has purchased once for $30.

You can build a high-value segment using criteria such as:

-   Total spend
-   Number of orders
-   Recent purchase activity
-   Average order value

Shopify supports customer filters based on attributes such as order count and customer data, allowing merchants to create targeted groups.

Send them:

-   VIP benefits
-   Early product access
-   Exclusive launches
-   Special experiences
-   Personalized recommendations
-   Loyalty rewards

The goal is not simply to **discount more**.

The goal is to make valuable customers feel recognized.

* * *

### 5\. Customers interested in a specific product category

Purchase history isn’t the only useful signal. You can also segment customers based on **what they browse, purchase, or interact with**.

For example:

**Skincare customers**

→ Skincare recommendations

**Coffee customers**

→ New coffee blends

**Running customers**

→ Running shoes and accessories

This is particularly useful when your store sells products across several categories.

#### The basic rule

**Don’t promote the entire catalog when you already know what the customer likes.**

Behavior-based segmentation can make your campaigns much more specific because the message is connected to actual customer activity.

* * *

### 6\. Inactive or at-risk customers

Some customers used to purchase regularly but have become inactive. This is where a **win-back segment** becomes useful. Instead of waiting until customers completely disappear, identify customers whose activity has dropped.

#### Possible criteria:

-   No purchase in 60 days
-   No purchase in 90 days
-   No email engagement recently
-   Previously purchased multiple times

The exact timeframe depends on your product’s normal buying cycle.

A customer buying consumables may become inactive after 60 days.

A customer buying furniture may still be perfectly normal after six months.

#### Send them:

1.  A reminder about your brand
2.  Relevant new products
3.  Personalized recommendations
4.  A customer-only offer, if needed
5.  A final re-engagement message

Shopify supports customer segments based on purchase and email activity, which can be used to target re-engagement campaigns.

* * *

### 7\. Highly engaged subscribers

Your most engaged subscribers are another useful group.

These customers may regularly open or click your emails, even if they haven’t purchased recently.

You can use email activity to identify groups based on events such as **opened, clicked, delivered, bounced, or unsubscribed** in Shopify’s segmentation system.

Send them:

-   New product launches
-   Educational content
-   Special announcements
-   Product recommendations
-   Limited-time campaigns
-   Early access

These subscribers have already shown that they want to hear from your brand.

The opportunity is to turn **engagement into purchase intent** without overwhelming them with unrelated promotions.

## Segment customers by lifecycle stage

Another simple way to organize your email strategy is by asking:

**“Where is this customer in their relationship with my store?”**

A basic lifecycle could look like this:

### Stage 1 — Subscriber

Has joined your email list but hasn’t purchased.

**Goal:** Get the first purchase.

↓

### Stage 2 — First-time buyer

Has purchased once.

**Goal:** Create a great post-purchase experience and encourage purchase #2.

↓

### Stage 3 — Repeat buyer

Has purchased multiple times.

**Goal:** Increase purchase frequency and customer value.

↓

### Stage 4 — Loyal customer

Frequently purchases and engages with your brand.

**Goal:** Build loyalty and reward the relationship.

↓

### Stage 5 — At-risk customer

Previously active but hasn’t purchased recently.

**Goal:** Re-engage before they become inactive.

↓

### Stage 6 — Lapsed customer

Hasn’t purchased or engaged for a long period.

**Goal:** Attempt reactivation, then reduce unnecessary messaging if they remain unresponsive.

This lifecycle approach makes it easier to decide **what to send and when to send it**.

## Use RFM segmentation for a deeper view

If your store has enough customer data, **RFM analysis** can provide another useful segmentation framework.

RFM stands for:

### Recency

How recently did they purchase?

### Frequency

How often do they purchase?

### Monetary

How much do they spend?

Instead of looking at only one metric, RFM combines these three signals to understand customer value and activity.

For example:

| Customer type | Recency | Frequency | Spend | Suggested message |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New buyer | High | Low | Low | Encourage second purchase |
| Loyal buyer | High | High | High | VIP / early access |
| At-risk buyer | Low | High | High | Win-back |
| Occasional buyer | Medium | Low | Medium | Product recommendations |
| Lapsed buyer | Very low | Low | Low | Re-engagement |

The advantage is that you’re not simply asking **“Who spent the most?”**

You’re looking at the customer’s **overall relationship with your store**.

## How to match each segment with the right message

Creating segments is only half the job.

The important question is:

**What should each segment actually receive?**

Here’s a simple framework:

| Segment | Main goal | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New subscriber | First purchase | Welcome + best sellers |
| First-time buyer | Second purchase | Education + related products |
| Repeat buyer | More purchases | Cross-sell + new products |
| VIP | Loyalty | Early access + exclusive benefits |
| Category shopper | Relevance | Category-specific products |
| At-risk customer | Reactivation | Win-back + recommendations |
| Engaged subscriber | Conversion | Product launches + targeted offers |

This is where segmentation becomes useful.

**A segment should always lead to a different message, offer, or customer experience.** If two segments receive exactly the same email every time, there may be little reason to keep them separate.

* * *

## Segmentation vs. personalization

These two terms are often used together, but they are not exactly the same.

### Segmentation

Groups customers together.

**Example:**  
Customers who bought running shoes.

### Personalization

Changes the message based on the individual customer.

**Example:**  
“Your Nike running shoes might pair well with these new running socks.”

Think of it this way:

**Segmentation = Who receives the message**

**Personalization = What the message says to them**

Using both together creates much more relevant campaigns.

## How to create customer segments in Shopify

Shopify provides a built-in customer segmentation system. You can access customer segments from:

**Shopify admin → Customers → Segments**

From there, you can create segments using filters and conditions. Shopify’s segment editor allows multiple filters to be combined using **AND** and **OR** logic.

For example:

### First-time buyers

You might create a segment around customers with:

**1 order + recent first purchase**

### VIP customers

You might use:

**High total spend OR multiple orders**

### Email subscribers

You can filter for customers whose email marketing status is **SUBSCRIBED**. Once created, Shopify segments can update automatically as customers meet or stop meeting the criteria.

* * *

## How to use segments in Shopify email campaigns

Once you’ve created a segment, you can use it as the audience for a marketing campaign.

The basic process is:

**Customers → Segments → Select segment → Use segment → Email**

Shopify Messaging allows merchants to create email campaigns for selected customer segments. Only customers who have subscribed to marketing emails are eligible to receive marketing messages through Shopify Messaging.

This distinction is important.

**Being a customer does not automatically mean someone has subscribed to marketing emails.**

Always respect the customer’s marketing consent and applicable privacy requirements.

* * *

## Segmentation + automation: where things get more powerful

Segmentation becomes even more useful when it connects with automation. Instead of manually checking segments and sending campaigns, you can create workflows that react when customer conditions change.

For example:

**Customer places first order**

→ Enters first-time buyer segment

→ Receives post-purchase email

→ Later receives relevant product recommendation

→ Makes second purchase

→ Moves into repeat-buyer segment

→ Receives loyalty-focused communication

Shopify also supports segment triggers that can start automated marketing when customers **join or leave a segment**. This turns segmentation from a simple filtering tool into part of an ongoing customer journey.

## Common Shopify email segmentation mistakes

### 1\. Creating too many segments

More segments don’t automatically mean better marketing. If you create 30 segments but don’t have different strategies for them, you’ve created more complexity without adding much value.

**Start with 4–7 useful segments.**

* * *

### 2\. Segmenting only by demographics

Age, gender, and location can be useful in some businesses. But **behavior often tells you more about purchase intent**. Someone’s recent purchase, browsing activity, and engagement can be stronger signals than basic demographic information.

* * *

### 3\. Sending the same promotion to every segment

If every segment receives:

**“20% off everything!”**

then segmentation isn’t doing much work.

Change the message based on the customer’s needs.

* * *

### 4\. Ignoring inactive subscribers

Continuing to send every campaign to people who haven’t engaged for a long time can reduce the relevance of your email program. Create a separate **re-engagement or sunset strategy** instead.

* * *

### 5\. Forgetting to update segments

Customer behavior changes. A first-time buyer eventually becomes a repeat buyer. An active customer can become inactive. Dynamic Shopify segments can update as customers’ information and behavior change, reducing the need to manually maintain lists.

## A simple Shopify segmentation strategy to start with

You don’t need a complicated segmentation system on day one.

Start with these five groups:

### 1\. New subscribers

**Goal:** First purchase

**Send:** Welcome series + best sellers

### 2\. First-time buyers

**Goal:** Second purchase

**Send:** Product education + related products

### 3\. Repeat customers

**Goal:** Increase loyalty

**Send:** New products + personalized recommendations

### 4\. High-value customers

**Goal:** Retain valuable customers

**Send:** VIP benefits + early access

### 5\. Inactive customers

**Goal:** Win them back

**Send:** Re-engagement + relevant recommendations

Once these are working, you can add more advanced segments based on product category, purchase frequency, email engagement, or RFM.

## How to know if your segmentation is working

Don’t judge segmentation only by open rates. Look at metrics that connect the campaign to business results.

Track:

-   Click-through rate — Are customers engaging with the message?
-   Conversion rate — Are they taking the desired action?
-   Revenue per recipient — How much revenue does each recipient generate?
-   Repeat purchase rate — Are customers coming back?
-   Unsubscribe rate — Are customers finding your emails irrelevant?
-   Engagement by segment — Which groups respond best?

The goal isn’t simply to get more people to open an email.

The goal is to **send a more useful message to the right audience and create a better customer journey**.

* * *

## Final thoughts

Good email marketing isn’t about sending more emails. It’s about making each email **more relevant**. Shopify email segmentation gives merchants a practical way to do that by grouping customers according to their behavior, purchase history, interests, and lifecycle stage.

Start simple:

**Segment → Understand the customer → Match the message → Automate where useful → Measure → Improve**

The most important rule is easy to remember:

> **Don’t ask every customer to do the same thing.**

A new subscriber needs a reason to buy.

A first-time customer needs a reason to return.

A loyal customer needs a reason to stay loyal.

And an inactive customer needs a reason to come back.

When each group receives communication that matches its situation, your email marketing becomes less like a broadcast and more like a **conversation with the customer**.
