A shopper reaches your checkout, enters their information, and then disappears before paying. For Shopify merchants, this can be frustrating because the customer was already **very close to placing an order**.

But an abandoned checkout does not always mean something is wrong with your store. Shoppers often use checkout to check the final price, compare shipping options, look for payment methods, or simply decide whether they’re ready to buy.

The real opportunity is to understand **where and why shoppers drop off**, fix the problems you can control, and recover customers who were genuinely close to purchasing.

* * *

## What is an abandoned checkout on Shopify?

An abandoned checkout happens when a shopper starts the checkout process but leaves without completing payment. Shopify distinguishes this from a simple abandoned cart, where someone adds products to their cart but never starts checkout.

This distinction matters because checkout abandoners have already taken more steps toward purchasing. They have usually selected products, reviewed their order, and started entering checkout information.

In Shopify’s current abandoned checkout system, a checkout can be recorded as abandoned after the customer has provided an email and the checkout remains incomplete for more than 10 minutes. Shopify also provides payment events in the checkout timeline, which can help merchants identify failed payment attempts.

> **The important point:** An abandoned checkout is not simply a lost customer. It’s a shopper who has already shown **strong buying intent**.

* * *

## Why do shoppers abandon Shopify checkout?

There isn’t one reason behind every abandoned checkout. Some shoppers were only comparing prices, while others encounter a real problem that prevents them from completing the order. The key is to separate **normal shopping behavior** from **checkout friction you can actually fix**.

### 1\. Unexpected shipping costs

One of the biggest reasons shoppers leave is discovering that the final cost is higher than expected.

A customer may see a $50 product, add it to their cart, and feel comfortable with the price. Then they reach checkout and discover an additional $10–$15 shipping charge. At that point, the customer may decide the order is no longer worth it.

This is why shipping information shouldn’t be treated as something customers only discover at checkout. If possible, communicate shipping costs, free-shipping thresholds, or estimated delivery times **before the customer reaches the payment page**. Both Recapture and Blockify highlight unexpected costs as a major source of checkout friction.

**What you can do:**

-   Show shipping information on product or cart pages.
-   Clearly communicate your free-shipping threshold.
-   Avoid unexpected fees appearing for the first time at checkout.
-   Make delivery estimates easy to find.

* * *

### 2\. Customers don’t trust the store enough

A shopper can like your product and still hesitate when it’s time to enter payment information. This is especially common for first-time customers who don’t know your brand yet. At checkout, they may start thinking about whether the store is legitimate, whether their payment is secure, or what happens if they need a refund.

Trust becomes particularly important when the order value is high. Make important reassurance easy to see:

-   **Secure payment options**
-   Clear return and refund policies
-   Customer reviews
-   Recognizable payment methods
-   Accessible customer support

You don’t need to fill the checkout page with badges and guarantees. A few relevant trust signals placed where customers are making their final decision can be much more useful.

* * *

### 3\. The preferred payment method isn’t available

Customers have different payment habits. Some prefer credit cards. Others expect PayPal, Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay, or another local payment method. If their preferred option isn’t available, some shoppers simply leave rather than change how they normally pay.

Payment problems can also create abandoned checkouts even when the customer genuinely wants to buy. Shopify notes that failed payments can result from issues such as declined cards, incorrect card details, risk blocks, technical problems, or failed 3-D Secure authentication.

**Don’t assume every abandoned checkout is a marketing problem.**

If payment attempts are failing, the solution may be to check your payment gateway, payment events, and available payment methods rather than sending more recovery emails.

* * *

### 4\. Checkout takes too much effort

Every additional step creates another opportunity for a customer to leave. This becomes even more important on mobile, where shoppers may have to type addresses, card numbers, and other information on a small screen. A checkout that feels slow, confusing, or unnecessarily complicated can turn a ready-to-buy customer into an abandoned checkout.

Look for simple ways to reduce friction:

-   Enable accelerated payment options where appropriate.
-   Make sure the checkout works smoothly on mobile.
-   Avoid asking for information you don’t actually need.
-   Use address autocomplete where available.
-   Keep important information clear and easy to understand.

The goal is simple: **make completing the purchase easier than leaving it unfinished.**

* * *

### 5\. Customers are looking for a discount

A coupon field can sometimes create a problem that merchants don’t expect. A shopper who wasn’t thinking about a discount may see a promo-code field and suddenly wonder whether other customers are paying less. They may leave checkout, search Google for a coupon, and never return.

This doesn’t mean you should never offer discounts. It means discounts should be used strategically rather than becoming the automatic solution for every abandoned checkout. If you regularly send a discount in every recovery email, customers may also learn to **wait for the discount instead of buying immediately**.

> 💡 **Better approach:** Start recovery with a simple reminder or helpful information. Use an incentive later only when it makes sense for your business.

* * *

### 6\. They’re still comparing options

Not every checkout abandoner was ready to buy. Some shoppers use checkout to calculate the final price, compare shipping, check delivery dates, or compare your offer with another store. Recapture describes this as one reason Shopify merchants can see many abandoned checkouts even when nothing is technically wrong.

This is why trying to eliminate **100% of abandonment** isn’t realistic. Your goal should be to identify the shoppers who were genuinely close to buying and give them a useful reason to return.

* * *

## How to find out why your customers are leaving

Before changing your checkout, look at your actual Shopify data. Go to **Shopify Admin → Orders → Abandoned checkouts** to review individual abandoned checkouts. Shopify also lets you inspect payment events in the checkout timeline, which can reveal whether a customer attempted payment and what happened. Look for patterns rather than individual cases.

If you see…

Many customers enter an address but don’t attempt payment  
→ Investigate shipping costs, taxes, delivery information, and unexpected fees.

Customers reach payment but repeatedly fail  
→ Check payment gateway settings, card errors, risk blocks, and payment events.

Customers abandon without reaching the payment stage  
→ Look at trust, checkout friction, pricing, and payment options.

One traffic source has unusually high abandonment  
→ Check whether that traffic is attracting shoppers with low purchase intent.

This approach is much more useful than simply looking at your total abandonment number.

## How to reduce abandoned checkouts before they happen

Recovery is important, but **prevention should come first**. You don’t need to redesign your entire Shopify store. Start with the areas most likely to create friction.

### 1\. Make the total cost clear

Don’t make customers wait until the final step to understand what they’re likely to pay. Show shipping information, free-shipping thresholds, and other important costs as early as possible. The more predictable the final price is, the less likely customers are to feel surprised at checkout.

* * *

### 2\. Offer convenient payment options

Make sure customers can use the payment methods that are relevant to your target market. Accelerated checkout options can also reduce the amount of information customers need to enter manually, which is especially useful on mobile.

* * *

### 3\. Allow customers to check out without unnecessary barriers

If your business doesn’t need an account before purchase, don’t make creating one feel like a requirement. Let customers complete their order first. You can always invite them to create an account afterward when there’s a clear benefit, such as tracking orders or managing future purchases.

* * *

### 4\. Build trust before the payment step

Don’t wait until checkout to answer important questions. Your product pages and cart should already make information such as **shipping, returns, guarantees, reviews, and payment options** easy to understand. By the time customers reach checkout, they should already feel comfortable buying from you.

* * *

### 5\. Test the checkout yourself

One of the simplest ways to find checkout problems is to actually place an order.

Test your store on:

-   Desktop
-   iPhone or Android
-   Different browsers
-   Different payment methods

Go through the process as if you were a first-time customer. If you find yourself wondering _“Where do I click?”_, _“Why is shipping this expensive?”_, or _“Can I pay with my usual method?”_, your customers may be asking the same questions.

## How to recover abandoned checkouts

Even with a well-designed checkout, some customers will still leave. That’s where recovery automation comes in.

Shopify provides abandoned checkout automation that can send customers a link back to their checkout. The current Shopify Messaging automation gives merchants control over the workflow and allows the email content, subject line, audience, and timing to be customized.

For stores that want more control, a multi-step recovery strategy can be more useful than relying on a single reminder.

## A simple recovery sequence

### **Email 1 — Friendly reminder**

Send a short reminder while the customer’s shopping session is still relatively fresh. Show the products they selected and provide a direct link back to checkout.

**Goal:** Remind, not pressure.

* * *

### **Email 2 — Remove hesitation**

If the customer still hasn’t purchased, address common concerns.

You could highlight:

-   Customer reviews
-   Shipping information
-   Return policy
-   Product benefits
-   Customer support

**Goal:** Give the customer the information they may need to make a decision.

* * *

### **Email 3 — Final reason to return**

If appropriate, offer a genuine incentive such as free shipping or a limited discount. Don’t automatically discount every abandoned checkout. The goal is to **recover the sale without training customers to abandon their carts for a coupon**.

**Goal:** Create one final reason to complete the purchase.

* * *

## Email isn’t the only recovery channel

Email gives you plenty of space to explain products, answer objections, and provide a direct checkout link. But it isn’t always the fastest way to get someone’s attention.

**Web push notifications can complement email** by providing a short, immediate reminder without requiring the customer to open their inbox.

A simple recovery journey could look like:

Customer abandons checkout  
↓  
Web push reminder  
↓  
Email with product + trust information  
↓  
Final reminder or incentive  
↓  
Purchase

For Shopify merchants using both channels, a platform such as **[Uppush](https://apps.shopify.com/pushup-notification-marketing?st_source=uppush_web&st_position=blog_post_body)** can help connect email and web push automation so recovery doesn’t depend on a single communication channel. The important part is not simply sending more messages. It’s making sure **each message has a different purpose**.

* * *

## Don’t send recovery messages blindly

Not every abandoned checkout should receive the same recovery sequence.

Shopify itself has conditions where an abandoned checkout recovery email may not be sent—for example, when the customer has completed another purchase before the email is sent, the items are no longer available, the store can’t ship to the customer’s address, or certain payment-related conditions apply.

This is another reason to monitor your recovery data rather than assuming every checkout is recoverable. You should also make sure your automation **stops when the customer purchases**. Nothing feels more disconnected than receiving an email saying _“Complete your order”_ after you’ve already completed it.

* * *

## A practical Shopify checkout recovery checklist

Before spending more money on traffic, make sure your checkout isn’t losing customers unnecessarily.

### **Checkout experience**

-   Shipping costs are clear before checkout.
-   Free-shipping thresholds are easy to understand.
-   Important return and refund information is easy to find.
-   Checkout works properly on mobile.
-   Customers aren’t forced to create an unnecessary account.
-   Relevant payment methods are available.
-   Accelerated checkout options are enabled where appropriate.

### **Recovery**

-   Abandoned checkout automation is active.
-   Recovery messages link directly back to checkout.
-   Product information is personalized.
-   The first message doesn’t rely on a discount.
-   Recovery stops after purchase.
-   Email and web push can work together where appropriate.

### **Measurement**

-   Review abandoned checkout patterns regularly.
-   Check payment events for failed transactions.
-   Compare abandonment by traffic source.
-   Track recovered orders and revenue.
-   Test different timing and messaging.

* * *

## Final thoughts

Abandoned checkout is a normal part of ecommerce. The goal isn’t to make every shopper complete their purchase—some visitors will always compare prices, think about their options, or decide to buy later. The bigger opportunity is finding out **why shoppers are leaving** and separating normal hesitation from problems you can actually fix.

Start with the basics: make costs clear, reduce unnecessary friction, provide trusted payment options, and make the checkout experience easy on mobile. Then build a recovery strategy for the customers who still leave.

_A shopper who reaches checkout has already shown you that they’re interested. Your job is to make it easy for them to come back and finish what they started._
