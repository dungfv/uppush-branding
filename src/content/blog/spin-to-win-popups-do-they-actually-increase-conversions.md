Spin-to-win popups have been around for years. Some marketers see them as an effective way to grow an email list, while others see them as an outdated gimmick that can make a store look cheap. The reality is somewhere in between.

**Spin-to-win popups can increase engagement and conversions, but the wheel itself is not what makes them work.** Timing, prizes, targeting, design, and what happens after the signup all have a much bigger impact.

So, are they still worth using? Let’s look at what makes them effective, where they fail, and how Shopify stores can use them without hurting the customer experience.

* * *

## What is a spin-to-win popup?

A spin-to-win popup is a gamified popup that gives visitors a chance to win a reward by spinning a digital wheel. The visitor usually enters an email address first, then spins the wheel to reveal a prize. Rewards can include discounts, free shipping, free gifts, or other incentives.

Instead of simply asking:

> **Get 10% off your first order**

the store turns the same offer into an interaction:

> **Spin the wheel for a chance to win up to 20% off**

That small change can make the signup feel more like participation and less like a marketing form.

* * *

## Do spin-to-win popups actually increase conversions?

**Yes, they can — but not automatically.**

Wisepops reports that spin-to-win popups can improve conversion compared with standard popup formats, while its examples include brands that used the format to accelerate lead generation.

Privy’s more recent analysis makes an important point: spin-to-win popups can still perform well in 2026, but they no longer work simply because they are novel. Their performance depends on **execution, timing, and offer quality**.

That distinction matters. A poorly designed wheel that appears immediately, offers weak prizes, and looks unrelated to the brand can easily perform worse than a simple email popup. Think of the wheel as a **conversion wrapper**. It can make an offer more engaging, but it cannot fix a bad offer or poor popup strategy.

* * *

## Why do spin-to-win popups work?

### 1\. They turn a decision into an interaction

A normal popup asks visitors to make a quick decision:

**Do I want to subscribe or not?**

A spin-to-win popup asks them to participate first. That interaction creates curiosity and gives visitors a reason to continue instead of immediately closing the popup. Privy describes this as a shift from forcing a decision to inviting participation. The difference is small, but it can make the experience feel more engaging.

* * *

### 2\. The reward feels earned

There is also a psychological difference between **being given** a discount and **winning** one. A 10% discount can feel like another generic promotion. But if the visitor spins and receives 10% off, the same reward can feel more exciting because they participated to get it. This is one reason gamification can make a familiar promotion feel fresh.

* * *

### 3\. It creates curiosity

The visitor does not know exactly what they will receive.

That uncertainty creates a simple question:

> **“What will I get?”**

The visitor has a reason to interact with the popup, rather than simply deciding whether they want another marketing message. This can be particularly useful for **first-time visitors who are still browsing**.

* * *

## When do spin-to-win popups work best?

Spin-to-win is not equally effective for every store. It tends to fit brands where customers are comfortable with promotions, discovery, and impulse purchases. Privy points to categories such as apparel, beauty, personal care, and lower-priced products as natural fits.

Hello Bar similarly recommends the format for mass-market ecommerce brands, particularly when email list growth is an important goal and the brand has enough margin to support the rewards.

### Good use cases include:

-   First-order offers
-   Email list growth
-   Seasonal campaigns
-   Black Friday and holiday promotions
-   Product launches
-   Fashion and beauty stores
-   Impulse-friendly products
-   Stores with frequent promotional campaigns

Seasonality can make the format feel especially natural because the game becomes part of the campaign rather than a random interruption.

* * *

## When should you avoid spin-to-win?

The format is not right for every brand. A luxury fashion store, professional service, or high-consideration business may create a very different impression with a colorful discount wheel.

Hello Bar identifies several situations where a spin wheel can be a poor fit, including premium brands, B2B businesses, professional services, research-focused websites, and businesses selling expensive products with limited catalogs.

### A standard popup may be better if:

-   Your brand depends heavily on **premium positioning**
-   Your products require significant education before purchase
-   Your customers are mainly **B2B buyers**
-   Your margins are too small to support frequent discounts
-   You want to avoid creating discount-dependent customers
-   Your main goal is collecting qualified leads rather than promotional subscribers

The key question is not **“Can I use a spin wheel?”**

It is:

**“Does this interaction make sense for my customers and my brand?”**

* * *

## 7 best practices for a spin-to-win popup that converts

### 1\. Don’t show it immediately

One of the biggest mistakes is displaying the wheel as soon as someone lands on the site. At that point, the visitor may not even know what you sell. They have no reason to exchange their email for an offer yet. Wisepops recommends delaying the popup or using exit intent, while Hello Bar suggests testing a delay of around 15–20 seconds or using exit intent.

#### Better triggers

-   **Time on page:** Give visitors time to browse.
-   **Scroll depth:** Show it after they demonstrate interest.
-   **Exit intent:** Use the wheel when someone is about to leave.
-   **Specific page targeting:** Show it on product or collection pages where the offer makes sense.

The goal is simple:

**Catch interest, not interrupt it.**

* * *

### 2\. Make the prize structure worth playing

The wheel needs to contain rewards that visitors actually care about.

A typical structure might include:

| Prize | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Small reward | 5% off |
| Useful reward | Free shipping |
| Medium reward | 10% off |
| Higher reward | 15–20% off |
| Non-discount reward | Free gift |

You do not need to make every prize a large discount. In fact, mixing **discounts, free shipping, gifts, and other rewards** can make the wheel more interesting while giving you more control over costs.

* * *

### 3\. Protect your margins

A spin-to-win campaign should increase revenue, not simply increase the number of coupons you give away. Higher-value rewards can have lower odds, while more affordable rewards can appear more frequently.

For example:

-   5% off → high probability
-   Free shipping → high probability
-   10% off → medium probability
-   20% off → low probability
-   Free product → very low probability

Hello Bar recommends deliberately structuring prize tiers so that exciting rewards create interest while lower-value rewards protect the economics of the campaign. The exact percentages should depend on your **product margins, average order value, and customer lifetime value**.

* * *

### 4\. Don’t include a “you lose” option

A visitor who takes the time to enter their information and play the game should receive something. Wisepops warns that “no prize” outcomes can create frustration, while Hello Bar recommends ensuring that every wheel segment provides some type of reward.

Instead of:

> Sorry, you didn’t win anything.

Use:

> **You won free shipping!**

Even a small reward makes the interaction feel positive.

* * *

### 5\. Ask for as little information as possible

The wheel is already asking visitors to complete an extra interaction. Adding multiple form fields can make the exchange feel like too much work.

A simple starting point is:

**Email address → Spin → Reveal prize**

Hello Bar specifically recommends keeping the initial form to a single email field. If you need additional information, collect it later through your email marketing or a separate preference form.

* * *

### 6\. Make the wheel look like part of your brand

A generic carnival-style wheel may immediately make your store feel less professional. Privy and Wisepops both emphasize the importance of brand-aligned design. The colors, typography, imagery, and overall style should feel connected to the website.

### Instead of:

🎡 Bright colors + random fonts + aggressive discount copy

Try:

**Your brand colors + familiar typography + clean product imagery + simple prize design**

The goal is for visitors to think:

> “This is part of the store.”

Not:

> “A random advertising widget just appeared.”

* * *

### 7\. Design for mobile first

A spin wheel that works on desktop can become frustrating on a phone. The wheel needs to be large enough to understand, the button needs to be easy to tap, and the popup should not make closing or continuing difficult. Wisepops specifically highlights responsive design as an important part of the experience.

Before launching, test:

-   Wheel size
-   Prize text
-   Spin button
-   Close button
-   Email field
-   Coupon display
-   Landscape and portrait layouts

**Do not simply shrink the desktop version for mobile.**

* * *

## What prizes should you offer?

Discounts are the obvious choice, but they are not the only option. The best reward depends on your products and margins.

| Prize | Best for |
| --- | --- |
| **5–10% off** | General ecommerce |
| **15–20% off** | Strong acquisition campaigns |
| **Free shipping** | Stores where shipping is a major friction point |
| **Free gift** | Beauty, food, lifestyle products |
| **Dollar discount** | Stores with predictable AOV |
| **Bundle upgrade** | Stores selling product sets |
| **Early access** | Product launches and loyal audiences |

Free shipping can sometimes be more attractive than a percentage discount because it removes a specific purchase barrier without reducing the product price itself. The important thing is to choose rewards that **feel valuable to customers without being unnecessarily expensive for your business**.

* * *

## Don’t let the wheel train customers to wait for discounts

This is one of the biggest long-term risks. If every new visitor sees a discount wheel, customers may start to believe that the normal price is not the real price. Hello Bar calls this **discount training**: repeated discount exposure can create an expectation that customers should wait for an offer before purchasing.

You can reduce this risk by:

-   Showing the wheel only to new visitors
-   Limiting how often someone sees it
-   Using free shipping or gifts instead of discounts
-   Running it only during specific campaigns
-   Testing different incentives
-   Offering early access instead of price reductions

A popup should support your pricing strategy, not undermine it.

* * *

## What happens after someone spins?

The spin should not be the end of the customer journey. If someone gives you their email address, you now have an opportunity to turn that signup into a purchase and eventually a repeat customer.

### A simple follow-up sequence could be:

**Immediately:**  
Send the reward and make the code easy to use.

**Later:**  
Recommend relevant products or bestsellers.

**After purchase:**  
Send product education, related products, or post-purchase content.

**Long term:**  
Move the customer into your normal email segments based on their behavior.

Hello Bar recommends sending the follow-up email within minutes so the customer can easily use the reward while the interaction is still fresh.

* * *

## Watch out for low-quality email signups

A spin-to-win campaign can attract people who only want the biggest prize. Some may enter fake or multiple email addresses to keep playing. Wisepops highlights this as a potential list-quality and deliverability problem.

To reduce the risk:

-   Use email verification when appropriate.
-   Limit the number of plays.
-   Add frequency caps.
-   Avoid extremely valuable prizes.
-   Monitor engagement after signup.
-   Remove consistently inactive subscribers over time.

**A larger email list is not automatically a better email list.** The quality of subscribers matters just as much as the number of new contacts.

* * *

## How to measure whether your spin-to-win popup works

Do not stop at popup conversion rate. A wheel can collect thousands of emails and still be a poor marketing investment if those subscribers never buy.

Track the full journey:

| Metric | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **Popup conversion rate** | How many visitors sign up |
| **Prize redemption rate** | How many winners actually use their reward |
| **Purchase rate** | How many subscribers become customers |
| **Revenue per subscriber** | How much revenue each captured contact generates |
| **90-day engagement** | Whether subscribers continue opening and clicking |
| **Discount cost per signup** | What each captured email costs |
| **Customer LTV** | Whether the campaign creates long-term value |

Hello Bar recommends looking beyond the initial capture rate and comparing subscriber quality, redemption, revenue, and the cost of rewards. This gives you a much clearer answer to the real question:

**Is the popup generating profitable customers, or just more email addresses?**

* * *

## Spin-to-win vs standard popup

If you are unsure which format to use, compare them based on your goal.

|  | Spin-to-win | Standard popup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engagement | **High** | Moderate |
| Gamification | Yes | No |
| Easy to understand | Moderate | **High** |
| Brand fit | Best for playful brands | **Works for most brands** |
| Discount flexibility | **High** | High |
| Premium positioning | Can be difficult | **Usually better** |
| Email list growth | Strong potential | Strong potential |
| Setup complexity | Higher | **Lower** |

The best answer may also be **both**.

For example, you could use a spin-to-win popup for new visitors browsing product collections, while using a standard content-focused popup on your blog. Hello Bar recommends matching the popup format to the visitor and the context instead of forcing one format across the entire website.

* * *

## The best way to test a spin-to-win popup

Instead of asking whether spin-to-win works **in general**, test whether it works for **your store**.

Start with two versions:

### Version A: Standard popup

> **Get 10% off your first order**  
> Join our email list and get your discount.  
> **\[Get my 10% off\]**

### Version B: Spin-to-win

> **Spin to win up to 20% off**  
> Enter your email for a chance to unlock your reward.  
> **\[Spin the wheel\]**

Keep the audience and traffic conditions as similar as possible.

Then compare:

1.  Signup rate
2.  Purchase rate
3.  Prize redemption
4.  Revenue per subscriber
5.  Subscriber engagement
6.  Discount cost

Hello Bar recommends running a controlled test rather than relying only on industry benchmarks.

* * *

## Final thoughts

So, **do spin-to-win popups actually increase conversions?** They can.

But the strongest results come from treating the wheel as part of a broader conversion strategy rather than as a magic conversion button. Privy’s 2026 analysis makes the same point: the format still works, but it no longer works simply because it is new.

A successful spin-to-win popup should:

-   Appear at the right moment
-   Offer rewards people actually value
-   Protect your margins
-   Match your brand
-   Work smoothly on mobile
-   Ask for minimal information
-   Follow up quickly
-   Measure revenue, not just signups

For the right Shopify store, especially in **apparel, beauty, lifestyle, and other promotion-friendly categories**, a well-designed spin-to-win popup can be a useful way to turn passive browsing into interaction, email signup, and eventually a purchase. The important part is not simply adding a wheel.

**It’s giving the right visitor a reason to spin.**
