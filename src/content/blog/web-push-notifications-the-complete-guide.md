Getting visitors to your Shopify store is only half the battle. The real challenge begins after they leave.

Some visitors browse your products and never come back. Others add items to their cart but don’t complete the purchase. Many are interested in buying—they simply get distracted, compare prices, or decide to wait. If you don’t reconnect with them, those potential customers may never return.

That’s where web push notifications come in.

Unlike email marketing, web push notifications let you send short, timely messages directly to a shopper’s browser. They don’t need to share their email address or install a mobile app. If they choose to receive notifications, you can remind them about abandoned carts, announce new arrivals, promote flash sales, or let them know when an out-of-stock product is available again.

In this guide, you’ll learn how web push notifications work, why they’re valuable for Shopify stores, when to use them, and how to build campaigns that increase engagement without annoying your customers.

* * *

## What are web push notifications?

Web push notifications are short messages that appear on a customer’s desktop or mobile browser after they choose to receive notifications from your website.

You’ve probably seen them before. You visit a website and your browser asks whether you’d like to allow notifications. If you click **Allow**, that website can send you updates even after you’ve left the page.

Unlike email marketing, customers don’t need to fill out a form or type their email address. The subscription process usually takes just one click, making it much easier for visitors to stay connected with your store.

For Shopify merchants, this creates another communication channel alongside email and SMS. Instead of relying on customers to check their inbox, you can send short, relevant messages that appear directly on their device.

* * *

### Web push notifications vs mobile app push notifications

These two terms are often confused, but they’re not the same thing.

**Web push notifications** work through a browser like Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari. Customers don’t need to install an app—they only need to visit your website and opt in.

**Mobile app push notifications** require customers to download your mobile app first. They are sent through Apple’s or Google’s app notification systems.

For most Shopify stores, web push notifications are much easier to adopt because they don’t require building and maintaining a mobile application.

* * *

### How do web push notifications work?

The process is surprisingly simple.

A visitor lands on your Shopify store and is asked whether they’d like to receive notifications. If they accept, their browser becomes a subscriber. From that point on, you can send notifications whenever they’re relevant, even if the customer isn’t currently browsing your website. Clicking the notification takes them back to the page or product you want them to see.

From the customer’s perspective, it only takes one click. From the merchant’s perspective, it creates an additional marketing channel that can be automated just like email campaigns.

* * *

## Why Shopify stores should use web push notifications

Web push notifications aren’t meant to replace email marketing. They’re designed to solve a different problem. They help you reconnect with visitors while your products are still fresh in their minds.

### Reach shoppers instantly

Emails can sit unread for hours or even days. Push notifications appear on a customer’s screen almost immediately after they’re sent, making them ideal for time-sensitive messages like flash sales, product launches, or cart reminders. If someone was already thinking about buying, a quick reminder at the right moment can be enough to bring them back.

* * *

### Recover abandoned carts

One of the most valuable uses of web push notifications is abandoned cart recovery. A shopper adds products to their cart, leaves your store, and receives a reminder shortly afterward saying their cart is still waiting. Unlike email, this reminder doesn’t require the customer to check their inbox. It appears directly in their browser and takes them back to checkout with a single click.

* * *

### Bring visitors back without paying for more ads

Paid advertising is becoming more expensive every year. Instead of spending more money attracting new visitors, web push notifications help you get more value from the traffic you’ve already earned. Whether visitors came from Google, Facebook, Instagram, or organic search, push notifications give you another opportunity to reconnect after they leave.

* * *

### Grow subscribers with less friction

Many shoppers hesitate before sharing their email address. Allowing browser notifications feels like a much smaller commitment because they don’t have to fill out a form or provide personal information. This simple subscription process often helps stores build their audience faster than relying on email sign-up forms alone.

* * *

### Increase repeat purchases

Web push notifications aren’t only for recovering lost sales. They also help bring existing customers back to your store. You can announce new collections, seasonal promotions, product launches, or exclusive offers to shoppers who have already shown interest in your brand. Keeping existing customers engaged is usually much more affordable than constantly finding new ones.

* * *

## Web push notifications vs email marketing

Many merchants wonder which channel performs better. The answer is that they work best together. Email gives you room to explain products, tell stories, recommend collections, and educate customers. Push notifications are much shorter and are designed to encourage immediate action.

For example, a push notification might remind someone that their cart is waiting. If they don’t return, an email later that day can answer common questions, explain your return policy, and include customer reviews.

Each channel supports the other rather than competing with it. This is why many Shopify brands now build multi-channel automation instead of relying on email alone.

## The best web push notification campaigns for Shopify

Not every notification should be promotional. The most successful campaigns are based on customer behavior.

### Welcome notifications

Welcome new subscribers with a friendly introduction to your store. This is a great opportunity to explain what type of notifications they’ll receive and, if appropriate, offer a first-purchase discount.

* * *

### Abandoned cart reminders

These reminders encourage shoppers to return and complete a purchase they already started. Keep the message short and include a direct link back to checkout.

* * *

### Browse abandonment

Not every visitor adds products to their cart. If someone repeatedly views the same product but leaves without taking action, a reminder can encourage them to continue shopping.

* * *

### Back-in-stock alerts

Out-of-stock products often create frustration. Instead of losing interested customers, notify them as soon as inventory becomes available again. This is one of the highest-performing automated notification types for ecommerce stores because customers have already shown purchase intent.

* * *

### Price-drop notifications

Some shoppers wait for a better deal before buying. Automatically notifying them when a product goes on sale helps recover demand without requiring customers to check your website repeatedly.

* * *

### Flash sales and limited-time offers

Push notifications work particularly well for promotions that only last a few hours. Because they appear immediately, customers are more likely to notice the offer before it expires.

* * *

### New arrivals

If customers have purchased from you before, they’re more likely to be interested in similar products. Announcing new collections through push notifications keeps your store top of mind and encourages repeat visits.

* * *

## Best practices for web push notifications

A successful push strategy isn’t about sending more notifications. It’s about sending better ones.

### Keep your messages short

People read push notifications quickly. Focus on one message, one offer, and one action. If you need to explain something in detail, email is usually a better channel.

* * *

### Send notifications at the right time

Timing has a significant impact on engagement. A cart reminder sent shortly after abandonment is much more relevant than one delivered several days later. Likewise, promotions should arrive when customers are most likely to see them rather than in the middle of the night.

* * *

### Personalize your messages

Generic notifications are easy to ignore. Whenever possible, mention the product customers viewed, the collection they’re interested in, or an offer that’s relevant to their shopping behavior. Personalization makes notifications feel helpful instead of promotional.

* * *

### Segment your audience

Not every subscriber should receive the same message. New visitors, first-time buyers, repeat customers, VIP shoppers, and abandoned carts all have different goals. Sending targeted notifications usually produces better engagement than broadcasting the same message to everyone.

* * *

### Don’t send too many notifications

More isn’t always better. Sending notifications too frequently is one of the fastest ways to lose subscribers. Shopify recommends being thoughtful about timing and cadence, and many marketers start with only a few notifications per week before adjusting based on engagement.

* * *

### Common mistakes to avoid

Many web push campaigns fail because merchants focus on quantity instead of relevance. Avoid sending notifications simply because you can. Every message should provide value to the customer.

Don’t rely only on discounts. Constant promotions train customers to wait for lower prices instead of buying immediately. Don’t ignore segmentation. Someone who abandoned a cart should receive a different message from someone who made a purchase yesterday.

Finally, don’t treat web push as a replacement for email. Both channels have different strengths, and they perform much better when used together.

* * *

## How to measure success

Like every marketing channel, web push notifications should be measured. Start with subscriber growth to understand how many visitors are choosing to receive notifications. Next, monitor click-through rate to see how many people return after receiving a notification.

If you’re using automated campaigns such as abandoned cart recovery, track recovered orders and recovered revenue to understand the real business impact. Also pay attention to unsubscribe rates. If too many people disable notifications, it may be a sign that you’re sending messages too frequently or that your content isn’t relevant.

* * *

### Build a complete customer journey

The most successful Shopify stores don’t rely on one marketing channel. A customer might discover your store through Google, subscribe to web push notifications, browse several products, abandon their cart, receive a reminder through a push notification, and finally complete their purchase after receiving a follow-up email.

Each touchpoint supports the next. Instead of thinking about web push notifications as individual campaigns, think of them as one part of a complete customer journey that helps shoppers move naturally from discovery to purchase.

## Final thoughts

Web push notifications have become one of the simplest ways for Shopify merchants to stay connected with shoppers after they leave the store. They require only a single click to subscribe, deliver messages instantly, and work particularly well for abandoned carts, flash sales, back-in-stock alerts, and product launches.

They won’t replace email marketing, and they shouldn’t.
